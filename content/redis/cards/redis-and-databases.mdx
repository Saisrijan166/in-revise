## Why it exists

Teams often ask whether Redis can replace their database. The answer depends on durability and query needs, not on speed alone.

## How it works

<Compare id="redis-vs-database" />

- How much Redis keeps after a crash: [RDB and AOF](card:persistence-rdb-aof).
- A lock alone does not make a payment exactly-once: [distributed locks](card:distributed-locks).

## Example

```text
-- PostgreSQL holds the payment row for PAY101. Redis only helps:
SET payment:PAY101:status PENDING EX 300   -- status copy, gone in 5 min
INCR rate:user:7:upi                       -- request counter
SET lock:payment:PAY101 w1 NX PX 30000     -- one worker at a time
```

## Interview Qs

### Can Redis be your primary database?

Yes, for data whose crash-loss window fits its persistence settings, such as sessions or leaderboards. For orders and payments, keep a relational database as the source of truth and add Redis for speed.

### When is Redis the wrong choice?

When the data is larger than RAM, or when you need ad hoc queries, joins, constraints and durable transactions across related records.
