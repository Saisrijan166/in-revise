## Why it exists

Many features need "the latest N things" or "the next job to do". A list adds and removes at the ends in constant time, however long it gets.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="A producer pushes new payment IDs on the left with LPUSH. The list runs from the newest item at the head to the oldest at the tail. A worker takes the oldest item from the right with BRPOP, so items are processed first in, first out."
  chart={`flowchart LR
  P[Producer] -- LPUSH --> H["head: PAY103, PAY102, PAY101 :tail"]
  H -- BRPOP --> W[Worker]`}
/>

- LPUSH with RPOP is first in, first out; LPUSH with LPOP is last in, first out.
- Reliable queues (LMOVE, BLMOVE) and when to pick a stream: [queues](card:queues-with-redis).

## Example

```text
LPUSH recent:merchant:42 PAY101 PAY102 PAY103  -- 3
LTRIM recent:merchant:42 0 99                  -- keep the 100 newest
LRANGE recent:merchant:42 0 9                  -- newest 10, newest first
BRPOP jobs:refund 5                            -- waits up to 5 s; nil on timeout
```

## Interview Qs

### Why are pushes O(1) but LINDEX slow?

Redis lists are linked lists, built for fast access at the head and tail. Reaching an item in the middle means walking the list, so it costs O(n). For fast access to the middle of a big collection, use a sorted set.

### What does BRPOP with timeout 0 do?

It blocks until an item arrives, with no time limit. BRPOP can watch several lists at once and returns two values: the list name and the item. With a non-zero timeout it returns nil when time runs out.
