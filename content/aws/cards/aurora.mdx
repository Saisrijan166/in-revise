## Why it exists

Classic read replicas each keep and update their own copy of the data. Aurora splits compute from one shared storage layer that grows by itself.

## How it works

<Compare id="rds-vs-aurora" />

- Aurora Serverless (named Serverless v2 until 04-2026) scales in half-ACU steps, 0-256 ACUs; 1 ACU is about 2 GiB of memory.
- [Aurora Global Database](card:aurora-global-database-node) adds read-only copies in other Regions; see [disaster recovery](card:disaster-recovery).

## Example

```properties
# writes: always the current primary, also after a failover
payments.writer.url=jdbc:postgresql://payments.cluster-abc123example.ap-south-1.rds.amazonaws.com:5432/payments
# reads: balanced across the Aurora Replicas
payments.reader.url=jdbc:postgresql://payments.cluster-ro-abc123example.ap-south-1.rds.amazonaws.com:5432/payments
```

## Interview Qs

### How is Aurora different from RDS PostgreSQL?

Same SQL and drivers, different storage. Aurora keeps six copies of the data across three AZs in a shared volume that grows by itself. Replicas read that volume, so they lag less and one is promoted faster on failover.

### Which endpoint should the payment service use?

The cluster endpoint for writes, because it follows the primary after a failover. The reader endpoint for reports and lookups that can be slightly stale; it spreads connections across the replicas.
