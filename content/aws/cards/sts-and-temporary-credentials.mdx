## Why it exists

Long-term access keys leak through code, images and logs. Short-lived keys that AWS issues and renews remove that risk.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="A caller (ECS task, EC2, Lambda or CI job) calls AssumeRole on STS. STS checks the role's trust policy and returns expiring keys. The caller signs an S3 call, and the role's permissions policy decides it."
  chart={`flowchart LR
  C["ECS task, EC2, Lambda or CI job"] -->|AssumeRole| STS[AWS STS]
  STS -->|"1. trust policy: may it assume?"| R[IAM role]
  STS -->|"2. temporary keys + expiry"| C
  C -->|"3. signed call"| S3[(S3)]
  S3 -.->|"4. permissions policy: may it act?"| R`}
/>

- On EC2, ECS, Lambda and EKS, AWS assumes the role; the SDK finds the keys via its [credential chain](card:aws-sdk-for-java).
- Cross-account: the other account's role trusts yours, and your policy allows `sts:AssumeRole` on it.
- On EKS, AWS recommends Pod Identity; IRSA also works on self-managed Kubernetes.

## Example

The payment service on ECS stores a receipt.

```java
// No keys here: on ECS the SDK uses the task role's temporary keys
S3Client s3 = S3Client.builder().region(Region.AP_SOUTH_1).build();

s3.putObject(PutObjectRequest.builder()
        .bucket("payments-receipts")
        .key("receipts/2026/10/pay_123.pdf")
        .build(),
    RequestBody.fromBytes(pdfBytes));
```

## Interview Qs

### How does an app on EC2 get AWS credentials without keys?

Attach an instance profile holding a role. The SDK reads temporary keys from instance metadata, and AWS rotates them before they expire. No keys on disk or in code.

### How do you let a service in account A read a bucket in account B?

A role in B trusts A and A assumes it, or B's bucket policy names A's role. A's own policy must allow the call too.
