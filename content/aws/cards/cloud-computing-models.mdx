## Why it exists

Owning servers means buying for the peak and paying for machines that sit idle. The cloud lets you rent capacity when you need it and hand it back.

## How it works

<Compare id="iaas-vs-paas-vs-saas" />

- Scale out by adding instances behind a [load balancer](card:elastic-load-balancing); [Auto Scaling](card:auto-scaling-groups) adds and removes them.
- How AWS charges for this, and the discounts: [AWS pricing](card:aws-pricing-models).

## Example

```text
Payment service, normal day : 2 instances
Sale day, 5x traffic        : Auto Scaling adds 8 more  -> 10
Night                       : scales in again           -> 2
You pay for 10 only during the peak hours (elasticity).
```

## Interview Qs

### IaaS vs PaaS vs SaaS, with AWS examples?

IaaS: EC2, you run the OS, runtime and app. PaaS: Elastic Beanstalk, you upload the JAR and AWS provisions servers, load balancing and scaling. SaaS: a finished app; you manage only your data and users.

### Vertical or horizontal scaling: which do you prefer on AWS?

Vertical (a bigger instance type) is simple but still one machine with a ceiling. Horizontal adds instances across AZs behind a load balancer, so one failure hurts less. It needs stateless services, with sessions in a cache or database.
