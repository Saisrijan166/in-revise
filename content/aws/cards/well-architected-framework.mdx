## Why it exists

Design answers drift towards one concern, often speed or cost. The framework gives AWS's checklist so security, reliability and the rest are not forgotten.

## How it works

| Pillar | Focus | On this site |
|---|---|---|
| Operational excellence | Run and monitor systems; automate changes | [CloudWatch](card:cloudwatch), [IaC](card:infrastructure-as-code) |
| Security | Protect data and systems; control permissions | [IAM](card:iam-basics), [KMS](card:kms-and-encryption) |
| Reliability | Work as intended; recover quickly from failure | [AZs](card:regions-and-azs), [DR](card:disaster-recovery) |
| Performance efficiency | Right resource types and sizes; watch performance | [Compute choice](card:compute-choice) |
| Cost optimisation | No unnecessary cost; scale without overspending | [Pricing](card:aws-pricing-models) |
| Sustainability | Less environmental impact; high utilisation | [Auto Scaling](card:auto-scaling-groups) |

- In a design interview, use the pillars as headings: [answer playbook](card:aws-architecture-playbook).

## Interview Qs

### What are the Well-Architected pillars?

Operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimisation and sustainability. Sustainability was added in December 2021, so "five pillars" is out of date.

### How would you apply it to the payment service?

Reliability: Multi-AZ RDS and instances in two AZs. Security: IAM roles, no stored keys, encrypted data. Cost: Savings Plans for the steady base. Operations: alarms and IaC. Then state the trade-off, such as paying for a standby database.
