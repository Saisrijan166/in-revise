## What it is

The AWS Well-Architected Framework is AWS's checklist for building systems on AWS. It groups best practices into six pillars, and for each pillar gives design principles, recommended practices and a set of questions you ask about your design. A Well-Architected review is a constructive conversation about your decisions and their pros and cons, not a pass-or-fail audit. AWS also offers the Well-Architected Tool, a console service that walks you through the questions at no charge.

## Why it exists

When you design a system, it is easy to focus on one concern, usually making it fast or cheap, and forget the others until something breaks or the bill arrives. The framework captures what AWS has learned from reviewing thousands of architectures, as a shared checklist, so security, reliability and the rest are considered on purpose. It gives a common language for a team to weigh a design, spot weak spots and decide which trade-offs are acceptable before going live.

## How it works

Use the six pillars as a checklist, one question at a time. For the payment service, each pillar maps to concrete choices on this site.

| Pillar | What it asks | On this site |
|---|---|---|
| Operational excellence | Can you run, watch and change the system safely? | [CloudWatch](card:cloudwatch), [infrastructure as code](card:infrastructure-as-code) |
| Security | Is data protected and access controlled? | [IAM](card:iam-basics), [KMS](card:kms-and-encryption) |
| Reliability | Does it keep working and recover from failure? | [AZs](card:regions-and-azs), [disaster recovery](card:disaster-recovery) |
| Performance efficiency | Are the resource types and sizes right? | [choosing compute](card:compute-choice) |
| Cost optimisation | Are you avoiding unnecessary spend? | [pricing](card:aws-pricing-models) |
| Sustainability | Are resources used efficiently, with less waste? | [Auto Scaling](card:auto-scaling-groups) |

1. Take your design and go through the pillars in turn, asking each pillar's questions about it.
2. For each answer, note where the design follows best practice and where it does not.
3. Where two pillars pull against each other, decide the trade-off on purpose and record it.
4. Fix the gaps that matter, or accept them knowingly. In an interview, the pillars make a ready structure for your answer: [the playbook](card:aws-architecture-playbook) uses them.

So the key is: a design is reviewed against all six concerns, and every trade-off is a stated choice.

## Rules and defaults

- **Six pillars, in this order often quoted:** operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimisation, sustainability. Sustainability was added on 02-12-2021.
- **A review is not an audit:** it is a conversation about decisions and their consequences, with no score.
- **Trade-offs are expected:** AWS says you make trade-offs between pillars based on your business context; security and operational excellence are generally not traded away for the others.
- **The WA Tool is free:** it provides the questions and recommendations; lenses add extra questions for a specific domain, such as serverless.

## Interview Qs

### What are the Well-Architected pillars?

Operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimisation and sustainability. Sustainability was added in December 2021, so answering "five pillars" is out of date. For each pillar AWS gives design principles, best practices and review questions you can ask about your own architecture.

### How would you apply the framework to the payment service?

Reliability: Multi-AZ RDS and instances in two AZs. Security: IAM roles, no stored keys, encrypted data. Cost: Savings Plans for the steady base load. Operations: alarms and infrastructure as code. Then I state the trade-off, such as paying for a standby database to gain availability.

### Is a Well-Architected review a pass-or-fail audit?

No. AWS describes it as a constructive conversation about architectural decisions and their pros and cons, not an audit. The goal is to understand where a design meets best practice, where it does not, and which trade-offs were chosen deliberately, then improve the parts that matter.
