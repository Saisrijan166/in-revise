## Why it exists

A single data centre can lose power or flood. AZs are separate buildings close enough for synchronous links, so one failure need not stop the app.

## How it works

| Layer | Multi-AZ means | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Compute | Instances in 2+ AZs, run by an Auto Scaling group | [Auto Scaling](card:auto-scaling-groups) |
| Load balancer | An ALB needs subnets in at least 2 AZs | [Load balancing](card:elastic-load-balancing) |
| Data | RDS Multi-AZ: a synchronous standby in another AZ | [RDS](card:rds) |

- Choose a Region by compliance first, then latency, price and the services it offers.
- Surviving a whole Region outage is [disaster recovery](card:disaster-recovery).

## Interview Qs

### Region vs Availability Zone?

A Region is a geographic area, such as Mumbai (`ap-south-1`), isolated from other Regions. An AZ is one or more data centres inside it with separate power and networking. AZs fail independently but are close enough for low-latency, synchronous replication.

### How do you choose a Region?

Compliance first: data residency rules can decide it. Then latency to most users, price (it differs by Region) and whether the services you need exist there.

### The payment app must survive an AZ failure. What do you check?

No single-AZ part: instances in 2+ AZs with enough capacity if one is lost, the load balancer in those AZs, RDS Multi-AZ, no state kept on one instance, and [network paths](card:internet-and-nat-gateways) in each AZ.
