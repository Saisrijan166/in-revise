## Why it exists

[Multi-AZ](card:regions-and-azs) survives a data-centre failure. Losing a whole Region, or having data corrupted or deleted, needs a plan agreed before it happens.

## How it works

| Strategy | RPO / RTO | In the second Region |
|---|---|---|
| [Backup and restore](card:dr-backup-restore) | Hours | Only backups; build everything after the event |
| [Pilot light](card:dr-pilot-light) | Tens of minutes | Data live; app servers switched off |
| [Warm standby](card:dr-warm-standby) | Minutes | A smaller, fully working copy, already running |
| [Multi-site active/active](card:dr-multi-site) | Near real time | Full copies serving traffic in every Region |

- Cost rises down the table. Data copies: see [replication options](card:dr-data-replication).
- Rebuild from [infrastructure as code](card:infrastructure-as-code); fail over with [Route 53](card:route-53).

## Example

```text
Need:    payment API, RPO 1 minute, RTO 15 minutes
Pick:    warm standby in a second Region
Data:    Aurora Global Database; S3 Cross-Region Replication for receipts
App:     a small ECS service already running there, scaled up on failover
Switch:  Route 53 failover record and health check, or an ARC switch
```

## Interview Qs

### What is the difference between RTO and RPO?

RTO is how long the service may be down before it is back. RPO is how much data you may lose, measured back to the last recovery point. A 1-minute RPO needs continuous replication, not nightly backups.

### Pilot light or warm standby?

Both keep data live in the second Region. Pilot light cannot serve until you turn on and scale its servers. Warm standby can take traffic at reduced size right away and only needs to scale up: faster, but it costs more.
