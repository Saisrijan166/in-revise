## Why it exists

Load is part steady, part spiky, part optional. One price for all three wastes money, so AWS trades discounts for commitment or for the right to interrupt you.

## How it works

## Example

```text
Payment service, as of 20-10-2026
  4 instances 24x7         -> Compute Savings Plan (also covers Fargate, Lambda)
  +6 on sale days          -> On-Demand, added by Auto Scaling
  nightly receipt rebuild  -> Spot; a job that is cut off just retries
```

## Interview Qs

### Savings Plans vs Reserved Instances?

Both are 1- or 3-year commitments with similar discounts. An RI discounts one instance configuration. A Savings Plan commits to USD per hour; Compute Savings Plans apply across family, size, Region, Fargate and Lambda. AWS recommends Savings Plans.

### When is Spot a bad idea?

For anything that cannot stop at two minutes' notice: a single stateful server, a database, a long job without checkpoints. Spot suits batch jobs, data analysis and background workers that retry.

### The AWS bill jumped. Where do you look?

Cost Explorer, grouped by service and usage type. Usual suspects: data transfer out, cross-AZ traffic, [NAT gateways](card:internet-and-nat-gateways), idle resources left running. Then set an AWS Budgets alert.
