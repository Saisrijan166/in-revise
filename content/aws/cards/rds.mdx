## Why it exists

On EC2 you patch, back up and fail over the database yourself. RDS takes those jobs.

## How it works

<Compare id="multi-az-vs-read-replicas" />

- The app connects to a DNS endpoint. On failover RDS points it at the standby, so keep the JVM DNS cache at 60 s or less.
- Point-in-time restore replays logs that RDS copies to S3 every five minutes.
- Many short-lived clients, such as [Lambda](card:lambda), should connect through [RDS Proxy](card:rds-proxy-node), which pools and reuses connections.

## Example

The JDBC URL and the password from Secrets Manager: [AWS Code Templates](card:aws-code-templates). Pool size per app instance:

```yaml
spring:
  datasource:
    hikari:
      maximum-pool-size: 10   # 6 app instances x 10 = 60 connections, below max_connections
```

## Interview Qs

### What happens to the app during a Multi-AZ failover?

RDS moves the endpoint's DNS record to the standby. Open connections drop, so the pool must reconnect, and the JVM must not cache the old IP for long.

### Rows were deleted by mistake at 10:42. How do you recover?

Restore to a point just before 10:42 from automated backups. RDS creates a new DB instance; copy the rows back or switch the app to it. A read replica would have copied the delete too.
