## Why it exists

Multi-step problems go wrong when a model jumps straight to an answer. Working through the steps first gives each step a chance to be right.

## How it works

| | Chain-of-thought prompt | Reasoning model |
|---|---|---|
| Who adds the steps | You, in the prompt | The model, built in |
| You control | Wording and worked examples | An effort level |
| Steps visible | Yes, in the reply | No; a summary at most |
| Cost | Longer visible output | Hidden reasoning tokens |

- Wei et al. (2022) showed CoT examples lift large models on arithmetic, commonsense and symbolic tasks.
- OpenAI sets `reasoning.effort`; Anthropic uses adaptive thinking with `effort`, replacing `budget_tokens`.
- Skip extra reasoning for simple extraction or classification: it adds cost and latency.

## Example

Example response: the app asks for a decision plus a short reason, not the thinking.

```json
{
  "orderId": "42",
  "eligible": true,
  "reason": "Returned 3 days after delivery; the example policy allows 7 days"
}
```

## Interview Qs

### What is chain-of-thought prompting?

Asking the model to write intermediate reasoning steps before the final answer. Wei et al. (2022) showed it improves large models on maths and logic tasks.

### Should you add "think step by step" for a reasoning model?

No. It already reasons internally, and OpenAI says such prompts are unnecessary and can hurt. Set the effort level and keep the prompt clear and direct.

### What do reasoning tokens cost you?

They are billed as output tokens, take context space and add latency, though you do not see them. They count toward the output limit, so a low limit can cut the reply.
