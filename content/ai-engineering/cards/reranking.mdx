## Why it exists

Fast retrieval must scan millions of chunks, so it compares pre-computed vectors. That is quick but can return chunks that are not really relevant.

## How it works

<Compare id="bi-encoder-vs-cross-encoder" />

- Options: a hosted [rerank API](card:hosted-rerank-api), an open [cross-encoder model](card:open-cross-encoder), or an [LLM as reranker](card:llm-reranker).
- Shortlist size and final top K are tuned in [retrieval tuning](card:retrieval-tuning).

## Example

"Where is my refund for order 42?" (numbers are an example, not a rule):

```text
hybrid search  -> 50 candidate chunks   (fast, approximate)
cross-encoder  -> scores 50 (question, chunk) pairs
keep top 5     -> into the prompt -> LLM answers
```

## Interview Qs

### What is reranking and why use it?

A second pass that re-scores retrieved candidates against the query and keeps the most relevant. Embedding search is fast but approximate; a cross-encoder reads both texts together and judges relevance better.

### Why not use a cross-encoder for the whole search?

It produces no vector to index, so it must run once per (query, document) pair. Over millions of documents that is far too slow; use it only on a shortlist.

### What does reranking cost?

An extra model call per query, with latency that grows with the shortlist. Keep the shortlist small and measure whether answer quality improves enough to pay for it.
