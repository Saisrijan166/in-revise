## Why it exists

All three improve answers, but they fix different problems at different costs. Choosing the wrong one wastes weeks, such as fine-tuning to teach facts that change every month.

## How it works

<Compare id="rag-vs-fine-tuning-vs-long-context" />

- OpenAI: not a fixed ladder. Start with a prompt and evals, then pull the lever the failures point to.
- Prefer RAG over long context when the corpus is large, mostly irrelevant per question, changes often, or needs per-user filtering.
- [Prompt caching](card:model-selection-and-cost) makes resending the same long prefix cheaper.

## Example

```text
"Answers use the old refund window"     -> knowledge -> RAG over the policy
"Order 42's refund status is wrong"     -> live data -> tool: getOrderStatus(42)
"Replies ignore our support tone"       -> behaviour -> prompt, then fine-tune
"Only a 6-page FAQ, rarely changes"     -> small    -> long context
```

## Interview Qs

### RAG vs fine-tuning?

RAG supplies knowledge at runtime: easy to update, can cite sources, no training. Fine-tuning trains on examples to change behaviour, such as format, tone or a narrow task. Knowledge goes to RAG, behaviour to fine-tuning; they can be combined.

### Context windows are huge now. Why still use RAG?

Sending everything costs tokens and time on every call, and long inputs can bury the key fact (see [context window](card:context-window)). RAG also filters per user. Anthropic notes that a knowledge base under about 200,000 tokens can simply go in the prompt.

### Can fine-tuning keep the model up to date?

Not reliably. Each change needs a new training run, and the model cannot show where a fact came from. See [training stages](card:llm-training-stages).
