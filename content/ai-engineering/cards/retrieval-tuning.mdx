## Why it exists

The raw question is often a poor search query, and stuffing every match into the prompt adds cost and noise. Tuning what goes in gives better, cheaper answers.

## How it works

<Steps label="From question to context">
  <Step title="Rewrite">Make the question standalone using the history; optionally expand it into several queries.</Step>
  <Step title="Retrieve">Search with top-K, a similarity threshold and the [tenant filter](card:metadata-and-filtering).</Step>
  <Step title="Trim">Drop duplicates; [rerank](card:reranking) and keep the best few.</Step>
  <Step title="Assemble">Order by relevance, label chunks with source ids, fit a token budget.</Step>
</Steps>

- Source labels make [citations](card:grounding-and-citations) checkable.
- History itself is kept by [conversation memory](card:conversation-memory).

## Example

```text
History:   "Where is my refund for order 42?" -> answered
Follow-up: "and order 43?"
Rewritten: "Where is the refund for order 43, and when will it arrive?"
Context:   [refund-policy#4] UPI refunds reach the customer in 5-7 working days.
           [payment-faq#2]   A refund shows as a credit on the UPI app.
```

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
|---|---|---|
| topK | 4 | Spring AI 2.0 `SearchRequest` |
| similarityThreshold | 0.0 (accept all) | Spring AI 2.0 `SearchRequest` |

## Interview Qs

### How do you pick top-K?

Measure it: run real questions and check whether the right chunk is in the top K and how much noise comes with it. Often: a larger K for search, then a reranker keeps a few.

### What is HyDE?

Hypothetical Document Embeddings (Gao 2022): an LLM writes a fake answer to the question, and that answer is embedded and searched instead, because it looks more like real documents than a short question does.

### The user asks "and order 43?" and retrieval returns nothing useful. Why?

Alone, the follow-up means nothing. Rewrite it with the history into a standalone query; Spring AI's `CompressionQueryTransformer` does this.
