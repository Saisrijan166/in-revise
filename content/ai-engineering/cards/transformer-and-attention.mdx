## Why it exists

Older [recurrent networks](card:neural-networks) read one word at a time, so training was slow and far-apart words were hard to link. Attention connects any two positions in one step.

## How it works

In "The customer asked about the refund because it was late", attention links "it" to "refund".

<Mermaid
  alt="For the token 'it', its query (what it is looking for) is compared with the key of every token (what each token offers). The match scores become weights that add up to one. The new meaning of 'it' is the weighted mix of all the tokens' values (the information each passes on), so most of it comes from 'refund'."
  chart={`flowchart LR
  Q["Query of 'it': what am I looking for?"] --> S["Compare with each key: what do you offer?"]
  S --> W["Scores to weights"]
  W --> V["Mix the values: what each passes on"]
  V --> N["'it' now carries 'refund'"]`}
/>

- Multi-head attention runs several of these in parallel; each head learns other links.
- Most chat LLMs are decoder-only: each token sees only earlier tokens. Encoder-only models such as BERT feed [embeddings](card:embeddings) and [rerankers](card:reranking).

## Interview Qs

### Explain attention without maths.

For each token, the model asks which other tokens help explain it, gives each a weight, and blends their information. That is how "it" picks up the meaning of "refund". Skip the formulas unless asked.

### Why did transformers replace RNNs?

An RNN must process tokens one after another. A transformer looks at all tokens at once, so training runs in parallel on GPUs and distant words connect directly.

### Why is a long context expensive?

Standard self-attention compares every token with every other token, so its cost grows with the square of the input length.
