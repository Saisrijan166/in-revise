## Why it exists

Embedding models have an input token limit, and one vector for a 100-page PDF matches everything weakly. Small chunks let search return the exact paragraph about UPI refunds.

## How it works

| Strategy | How it splits | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed size | every N tokens, with overlap | plain text, a quick start |
| Sentence-aware | fixed size, but cut at `.`, `?`, `!` or a newline | most prose |
| By structure | headings, sections, pages | policies, FAQs, Markdown, HTML |
| Semantic | where the meaning changes | long text that mixes topics |

- Overlap keeps a sentence cut at a boundary whole in at least one chunk; OpenAI caps it at half the chunk size.
- A chunk such as "it takes 5-7 days" means little alone: add the document title or section to it, or use [contextual retrieval](card:contextual-chunk-context).

## Example

```text
Chunk 1: ... Refunds go back to the original payment method.
         UPI refunds reach the customer in 5-7 working days.
Chunk 2: UPI refunds reach the customer in 5-7 working days.   <- overlap
         Card refunds can take up to 10 working days. ...
(fictional example policy)
```

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Chunk size / overlap | 800 / 400 tokens | OpenAI file search (max chunk 100-4,096 tokens) |
| Chunk size | 800 tokens, cut at sentence ends | Spring AI 2.0 `TokenTextSplitter` |

## Interview Qs

### How do you choose chunk size?

Start from a documented default, check the document shapes, then measure retrieval recall on real questions and adjust. Smaller suits precise facts; larger keeps context.

### Why overlap, and what does it cost?

It keeps an answer split across a boundary. It costs more chunks, storage and embedding calls, and near-duplicates in results.
