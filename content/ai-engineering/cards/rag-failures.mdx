## Why it exists

"The bot gave a wrong refund date" has many possible causes. Naming the failure type tells you which part of the pipeline to fix, instead of guessing.

## How it works

| Failure | Refund example | Likely fix |
|---|---|---|
| Data | Policy chunk still says 10 days | Fix and [re-index](card:rag-pipeline) the document |
| Retrieval | "UPI refund" chunk not in the results | [Chunking](card:chunking), [filters](card:metadata-and-filtering), [hybrid search](card:hybrid-search) |
| Ranking | Found at rank 9, K is 5 | [Reranking](card:reranking), [top-K](card:retrieval-tuning) |
| Context | Right chunk among 20 noisy ones | Fewer, better chunks |
| Generation | Chunk present, model says "3 days" | Prompt, [grounding](card:grounding-and-citations) |

- Measure both sides in [evals](card:ai-evaluation): context recall and precision for retrieval, faithfulness for the answer.

## Interview Qs

### Your RAG bot gives wrong answers. How do you debug it?

Look at what was retrieved for failing questions. Right chunk missing: fix data, chunking, filters or search. Ranked low: rerank or adjust K. Present but misused: fix the prompt and grounding. Add each case to evals.

### How do you improve a RAG system?

In order: clean documents, better chunking, a suitable embedding model, metadata filters, hybrid search, reranking, a grounded prompt with citations, and evals that show whether each change helped.

### Is RAG ever "done"?

No. Barnett et al. found a RAG system is validated in operation and its robustness grows over time, so monitor real questions and keep extending the eval set.
