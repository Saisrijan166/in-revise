## Why it exists

Computers cannot compare meaning in words directly. Numbers they can compare, so "refund not received" can find "money not back yet" with no shared keyword.

## How it works

<Compare id="embeddings-vs-llm" />

- Text goes to an [embedding model](card:embedding-model); a [vector](card:vector) of fixed length comes back.
- Closeness is measured with [cosine similarity](card:similarity-search); vectors are stored in a [vector database](card:vector-databases).
- The [dimension](card:embedding-dimension) must match the database column; a new model often means a new column.

## Example

Illustrative numbers; real vectors have hundreds or thousands of values.

```text
"refund not received"   -> [ 0.12, -0.45, 0.73, ...]  close
"money not back yet"    -> [ 0.10, -0.41, 0.70, ...]  close
"change my password"    -> [-0.66,  0.32, 0.05, ...]  far
```

In Spring AI, `EmbeddingModel.embed(String)` returns the vector as a `float[]`.

## Interview Qs

### What are embeddings?

Vectors of numbers that capture the meaning of text, images or other data. Items with similar meaning get vectors that sit close together, which makes search by meaning possible.

### Can you switch embedding models without re-indexing?

No. Two models build different vector spaces, so their vectors cannot be compared. Re-embed every document with the new model, often into a new column, then switch queries over.

### Why not use the chat LLM for search?

A chat LLM returns text, not a vector you can index. An embedding model returns one vector per chunk, computed once at ingestion, so each search only embeds the question and compares vectors.
