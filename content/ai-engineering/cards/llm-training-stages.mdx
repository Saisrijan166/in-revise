## Why it exists

Next-token prediction alone gives a model that continues text but does not follow requests. Later stages turn it into a helpful assistant, and fine-tuning adapts it to one product.

## How it works

<Steps label="From raw text to a chat assistant">
  <Step title="Pre-training">Predict the next token over huge text; no labels needed. Result: a base model.</Step>
  <Step title="Supervised fine-tuning">Learn from written demonstrations of good answers. Result: it follows instructions.</Step>
  <Step title="Preference tuning">People rank answers. RLHF trains a reward model and optimises against it; DPO learns from the rankings directly.</Step>
  <Step title="Your fine-tune (optional)">Adapt tone, format or a narrow task, often with LoRA on an open-weight model.</Step>
</Steps>

- LoRA, on GPT-3 175B, cut trainable parameters about 10,000 times (Hu et al., 2021).
- Provider fine-tuning APIs change: OpenAI docs (21-10-2026) say its fine-tuning platform is winding down.
- Which to choose for a feature: [RAG or fine-tuning](card:rag-or-fine-tuning).

## Interview Qs

### What is RLHF?

Reinforcement learning from human feedback. People rank model answers, a reward model learns those preferences, and the model is tuned to score well. InstructGPT used it after supervised fine-tuning.

### When would you fine-tune instead of prompting?

When prompts and examples cannot get a consistent format or tone, or to make a smaller, cheaper model good at one narrow task. Not to add facts that change.

### What is LoRA?

A parameter-efficient fine-tuning method. The base weights stay frozen and only small low-rank matrices are trained, so it needs far less GPU memory and storage.
