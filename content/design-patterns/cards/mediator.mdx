## Why it exists

On a checkout screen, the cart, coupon box, wallet balance and Pay button all affect each other. Direct calls between them grow into a tangle nobody can change safely.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="A CheckoutMediator sits in the middle. The cart, the coupon box and the wallet balance each send changes to the mediator, and the mediator enables or disables the Pay button. No component calls another directly."
  chart={`flowchart LR

  K[Coupon box] -- applied --> M

- A colleague calls one notify method on the mediator, passing what happened.
- The mediator holds the rules: "total is ₹500, wallet has ₹300, so disable wallet pay".
- Over time a mediator can grow into a [god object](card:anti-patterns); keep one per screen or flow.

## Interview Qs

### How is Mediator different from Facade?

A [facade](card:facade) gives outside callers a simpler entry; the subsystem does not know it exists and its parts may still call each other. A mediator sits among peers that know it and only talk through it.

### How is Mediator different from Observer?

Mediator removes mutual links between a group of objects. Observer sets up one-way subscriptions from one subject to many observers. A common build uses both: the mediator publishes events and colleagues subscribe.
