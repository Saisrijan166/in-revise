## Why it exists

When a node runs out of memory or disk, something must go. QoS classes and priorities decide which Pods are given up to keep the node alive.

## How it works

<Steps label="Node-pressure eviction on one node">
  <Step title="Detect">The kubelet sees a signal cross a threshold, such as free memory below 100Mi.</Step>
  <Step title="Reclaim">It frees what it can first; for disk, dead containers and unused images.</Step>
  <Step title="Rank">Pods using more than their requests go first, by priority, then by how far over they are.</Step>
  <Step title="Evict">The Pod's phase becomes Failed (reason Evicted); its controller creates a replacement Pod.</Step>
</Steps>

- The kubelet does not rank by QoS class; the class only predicts this order for memory.
- A [PriorityClass](card:priorityclass) gives Pods a number; the scheduler preempts lower-priority Pods to fit a higher one.
- Debugging a killed or evicted Pod: [Pod errors](card:pod-errors).

## Interview Qs

### Which Pods go first when a node runs low on memory?

BestEffort and Burstable Pods using more than their requests, ordered by priority and then by how far over they are. Guaranteed Pods and Pods within their requests go last.

### How do you protect a critical Pod?

Make it Guaranteed (requests equal limits for CPU and memory in every container), keep its usage within requests, and give it a high PriorityClass.

### Does a PodDisruptionBudget stop evictions?

Only API-initiated ones, such as `kubectl drain`. Node-pressure eviction ignores PDBs and the grace period, and preemption respects PDBs only on a best-effort basis. See [high availability](card:high-availability).
