## Why it exists

Stopping every Pod to upgrade means downtime. Replacing a few at a time keeps payment-service serving; old ReplicaSets make going back quick.

## How it works

<Steps label="payment-service 1.4.0 to 1.5.0, 3 replicas, default 25% settings">
  <Step title="Work out limits">25% of 3: maxSurge rounds up to 1, maxUnavailable down to 0. At most 4 Pods, at least 3 available.</Step>
  <Step title="Surge">The new ReplicaSet starts one 1.5.0 Pod.</Step>
  <Step title="Swap">When it is Ready, the old ReplicaSet stops one 1.4.0 Pod.</Step>
  <Step title="Repeat">Until 3 new Pods run; the old ReplicaSet stays at 0 as the previous revision.</Step>
</Steps>

- Readiness decides when a new Pod counts: see [probes](card:probes).
- Blue-green and canary need more than a Deployment: see [release strategies](card:release-strategies).

## Example

```bash
kubectl -n payments set image deployment/payment-service payment-service=payment-service:1.5.0
kubectl -n payments annotate deployment/payment-service kubernetes.io/change-cause="1.5.0: UPI retry fix"
kubectl -n payments rollout status deployment/payment-service
kubectl -n payments rollout history deployment/payment-service
kubectl -n payments rollout undo deployment/payment-service      # or --to-revision=2
kubectl -n payments rollout restart deployment/payment-service
```

## Interview Qs

### What do maxSurge and maxUnavailable mean with 4 replicas?

With the 25% defaults both become 1: up to 5 Pods, at least 3 available. maxSurge 1 with maxUnavailable 0 never loses capacity, but needs room for one extra Pod.

### A rollout is stuck. What happens, and what do you do?

After progressDeadlineSeconds it reports ProgressDeadlineExceeded; nothing is rolled back. Check the new Pods' events, then fix forward or run `kubectl rollout undo`.

### When would you choose Recreate?

When two versions must never run together, such as an incompatible schema change. It costs a short outage.
