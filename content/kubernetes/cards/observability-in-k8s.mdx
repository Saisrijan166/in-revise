## Why it exists

Pods come and go, so you cannot log in and read files. You need logs that outlive Pods, a record of cluster actions, and metric history.

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Event retention | 1 hour (`--event-ttl`) | kube-apiserver, Kubernetes 1.37 |
| Container log rotation | 10Mi per file, 5 files | kubelet, Kubernetes 1.37 |
| metrics-server collection | Every 15 seconds | metrics-server (kubernetes-sigs) |

## Interview Qs

### A container keeps crashing. Where are its logs, and how do you keep them?

`kubectl logs --previous` shows the last crashed container; the kubelet keeps one. When the Pod is deleted or evicted, its logs go too. Ship them with a node agent run as a [DaemonSet](card:daemonsets).

### metrics-server or Prometheus: which one for what?

metrics-server holds only the latest CPU and memory values; HPA, VPA and `kubectl top` use them. Prometheus scrapes and stores time series, runs PromQL queries and alerts; Grafana draws the dashboards.
