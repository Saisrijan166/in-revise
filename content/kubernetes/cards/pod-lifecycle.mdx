## What it is

The Pod lifecycle is the path a [Pod](card:pods) takes from creation to deletion. Its phase is a one-word summary in the Pod's status: Pending, Running, Succeeded, Failed or Unknown. Each container inside also has a state: Waiting, Running or Terminated. Graceful shutdown is the fixed order Kubernetes follows when it stops a Pod, so the app can finish its work before it is killed.

## Why it exists

Pods are stopped all the time: during a new release, when a Deployment scales down, or when a node is drained for maintenance. If Kubernetes killed the process at once, payment-service would drop payments it was halfway through. The fixed order gives the app a warning signal (SIGTERM), time to finish, and only then a hard kill (SIGKILL). The phases tell you, at a glance, whether a Pod is waiting, working or finished.

Follow one payment-service Pod through its life:

1. **Pending:** the API server accepted the Pod. It waits for the scheduler to pick a node, then for the node to pull the image.
2. **Running:** the Pod is bound to a node and its containers are created. If a container crashes, the kubelet restarts it in place, waiting 10 s, then 20 s, 40 s and so on, up to 5 minutes.
3. **Delete:** you delete the Pod, or a rollout replaces it. The Pod is marked Terminating, and in parallel Services stop sending it new requests (you will see [Services](card:services) in the next topic).
4. **preStop:** if the container has a preStop hook, the kubelet runs it first. The grace period clock is already running.
5. **SIGTERM:** the kubelet sends SIGTERM to process 1 in each container. The app stops taking new work, finishes what it has, and exits.
6. **SIGKILL:** if a container is still running when the grace period (30 s by default) ends, it is killed. The Pod ends as Succeeded or Failed.

So the key is: SIGTERM is the polite request, and the grace period is your deadline.

## Example

payment-service gets 45 s to stop, with a short preStop pause first:

```yaml
spec:
  terminationGracePeriodSeconds: 45   # preStop 10 s + Spring Boot 30 s + 5 s margin
  containers:
    - name: payment-service
      image: payment-service:1.4.0
      lifecycle:
        preStop:
          sleep: { seconds: 10 }      # keep serving while endpoint removal spreads
```

What to notice:

- Removing the Pod from Services and sending signals happen at the same time. The 10 s preStop sleep keeps the app serving requests that are still on their way, instead of refusing them.
- Spring Boot's graceful shutdown waits up to 30 s for in-flight requests after SIGTERM. 10 + 30 = 40 s, so the default grace period of 30 s would cut it off; 45 s leaves a margin.
- Delete the `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` line and the default 30 s applies: preStop takes 10 s, and SIGKILL may arrive while payments are still being saved.

## Rules and defaults

| What | Default | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Grace period | 30 seconds | Kubernetes 1.37, `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` |
| restartPolicy | Always | Kubernetes 1.37 Pod spec |
| Restart back-off | 10 s, doubling, capped at 300 s; reset after 10 min running | Kubernetes 1.37 kubelet |
| Web server shutdown | graceful, up to 30 s | Spring Boot 4.1, `server.shutdown`, `spring.lifecycle.timeout-per-shutdown-phase` |

- **Allowed values:** a Deployment's Pod template allows only `Always`; a Job allows only `OnFailure` or `Never`.
- **preStop must end:** SIGTERM is sent only after the preStop hook finishes. If the hook outlives the grace period, the kubelet gives one extra 2 s, then kills the container.
- **Force delete:** `kubectl delete pod --grace-period=0 --force` removes the Pod object at once, without waiting for the node to confirm the container stopped.

## Common mistakes

- **"CrashLoopBackOff is a Pod phase."** It is a status kubectl shows while the kubelet waits before the next restart; the phase is still Running. The causes and fixes are in [Common Pod Errors](card:pod-errors).
- **"My app does not handle SIGTERM, but the grace period protects it."** An app that ignores SIGTERM just runs until SIGKILL and stops mid-request. The app itself must drain on SIGTERM, as Spring Boot's graceful shutdown does.

## Interview Qs

### How does Kubernetes stop a Pod gracefully?

It marks the Pod Terminating and, in parallel, removes it from Service endpoints. The kubelet runs any preStop hook, then sends SIGTERM to each container's main process and waits up to `terminationGracePeriodSeconds`, 30 s by default. Anything still running then gets SIGKILL. The grace period includes the preStop time.

### A Pod shows Running but users get errors. Why?

Running only means the containers were created and one is running. The app may still be starting, or be broken. A readiness probe keeps such Pods out of Service traffic until they answer correctly; see [Probes](card:probes).

### Why add a preStop sleep if the app already handles SIGTERM?

Endpoint removal and SIGTERM happen at the same time, and other nodes may route traffic to the Pod for a few more seconds. A short preStop sleep keeps the app serving those late requests before shutdown starts. Raise the grace period to cover the sleep.
