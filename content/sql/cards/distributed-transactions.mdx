## Why it exists

An order may touch an orders database, a payments database and a message broker. No local transaction spans them, so a crash halfway leaves them disagreeing.

## How it works

<Compare id="two-phase-commit-vs-saga" />

- Saga styles: [choreography](card:choreography-saga) (services react to events) or [orchestration](card:orchestration-saga) (one coordinator gives orders).
- The [outbox](card:transactional-outbox) relay may send an event twice, so consumers must be idempotent.

## Example

```sql
BEGIN;
INSERT INTO orders (id, user_id, amount, status)
VALUES (42, 7, 999.00, 'PENDING');
INSERT INTO outbox (aggregate_id, event_type, payload)
VALUES (42, 'OrderCreated', '{"orderId": 42}');
COMMIT;   -- both rows or neither; a relay publishes the outbox row later
```

## Interview Qs

### In 2PC, what if the coordinator crashes after everyone prepared?

Participants stay prepared and keep their locks. They cannot commit or roll back alone; they wait until the coordinator, or an operator, finishes each one with COMMIT PREPARED or ROLLBACK PREPARED.

### Why do microservices use sagas instead of 2PC?

Each service owns its database, and many brokers and NoSQL stores do not support 2PC. 2PC also adds latency and blocking. A saga needs only local transactions plus messages.
