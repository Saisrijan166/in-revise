## Why it exists

One server is a single point of failure and has limited read capacity. Copies on other servers can take over after a crash and share the reads.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="The app sends writes to the primary. The primary writes its log (WAL in PostgreSQL, binary log in MySQL) and the log is streamed to two replicas. The app sends reads to the replicas."
  chart={`flowchart LR
  App -- writes --> P[(Primary)]
  P -- "WAL / binlog" --> R1[(Replica 1)]
  P -- "WAL / binlog" --> R2[(Replica 2)]
  App -. reads .-> R1
  App -. reads .-> R2`}
/>

- PostgreSQL: [streaming replication](card:streaming-replication) copies the whole server; [logical replication](card:logical-replication) copies chosen tables.
- MySQL: replicas pull the source's binary log; GTIDs mark each transaction.
- To stop losing commits on a crash, make it synchronous: [Sync vs Async](compare:sync-vs-async-replication).

## Interview Qs

### A user updates a profile and the page still shows old data. Why?

The read went to a replica that had not yet applied the write (replication lag). Read that user's data from the primary right after a write, or pin the session to the primary.

### Does adding replicas scale writes?

No. Every write still goes to the one primary, and each replica must apply every write too. Replicas scale reads and availability; to scale writes, see [sharding](card:sharding).

### Is a replica a backup?

No. It copies mistakes at once, such as a bad DELETE. Keep real backups with [point-in-time recovery](card:backup-recovery).
