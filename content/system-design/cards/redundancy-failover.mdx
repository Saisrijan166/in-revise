## What it is

Redundancy means running more than one copy of each important part, so that if one copy fails, a spare is ready to take its place. Failover is the act of moving traffic from a failed copy to a healthy one. Our growing payment service starts on one server; the moment that server dies, the whole service is down. Redundancy and failover are how you stop one dead machine from stopping everyone.

## Why it exists

Every server, disk, network link and data centre fails sometimes. If only one copy of a part exists, that part is a [single point of failure](term:single-point-of-failure): its failure takes the whole system down, however reliable everything else is. Running a second copy, in a place that fails separately, means a failure of one copy is survivable instead of fatal.

## How it works

<Steps label="Automatic failover in four steps">
<Step title="Detect">
Health checks or heartbeats to a copy fail several times in a row. A short grace period avoids failing over just because a node was briefly busy.
</Step>
<Step title="Promote one">
A tool or a person makes one standby the new primary. For a whole region, teams often trigger this themselves, because a false alarm also causes downtime.
</Step>
<Step title="Fence the old one">
The old primary is fenced off, meaning it is forcibly stopped or blocked from writing, so two nodes never both accept writes.
</Step>
<Step title="Redirect">
A load balancer, a moved IP address or a DNS record now points at the new primary. Clients holding a cached DNS answer keep using the old one until its [TTL](term:ttl) runs out.
</Step>
</Steps>

So the key is that copies must fail separately: put them in different [zones](card:multi-az) or [regions](card:multi-region), not two servers on one rack.

## Example

How long until users reach the standby when failover is driven by DNS? With Amazon Route 53 defaults:

- **Detect:** a health check every 30 seconds, marked unhealthy after 3 failures in a row, so about 90 seconds.
- **Redirect:** resolvers may keep serving the old answer until its record TTL ends; AWS advises a TTL of 60 seconds or less, so up to 60 seconds more.

What to notice:

- The total is about 2.5 minutes before most clients reach the standby, so your [RTO](term:rto) must be at least that long for DNS-based failover.
- Lowering the health-check interval and the DNS TTL shortens both parts, at the cost of more checks and more DNS lookups.
- A load balancer in front of the servers redirects faster than DNS, because clients do not cache its routing decisions.

## Rules and defaults

- **Amazon RDS Multi-AZ failover:** typically 60-120 seconds; the standby is promoted and the database endpoint is moved to it.
- **Amazon RDS Multi-AZ standby:** it does not serve reads; it only waits to take over. To offload reads you add separate read replicas.
- **MongoDB 9.0 replica set:** an election starts when the primary is unreachable for about 10 seconds; the median election completes in 12 seconds or less.
- **PostgreSQL 18:** failure detection and failover are not built in; you add an external tool to detect failure and promote a standby.

## Not to be confused with

Redundancy is having spare copies; failover is the switch to one of them. Replication (copying data between nodes) is what keeps a standby's data current, but on its own it does not detect failure or redirect traffic. Rule: replication keeps a copy ready, failover is the act of using it.

## Common mistakes

- **"Two copies are enough, wherever they sit."** Two servers on one rack or in one zone still share a power supply or a network switch. Copies must sit in independent [failure domains](term:failure-domain) to fail separately.
- **"A split brain is rare, so we can ignore it."** After a network split, both sides may try to be primary and accept conflicting writes. You must prevent it with a majority [quorum](card:quorum) and by fencing the loser.

## Interview Qs

### How do you remove single points of failure from a design?

List every part on the request path: DNS, load balancer, app servers, cache, database. Run each at least twice, in different zones. Stateless parts go active-active behind a load balancer; the database gets a standby with automatic failover and fencing.

### What is split brain, and how do you prevent it?

After a network split, two nodes both act as primary and accept writes, so their data diverges and cannot be cleanly merged. Prevent it with a majority vote, where only the side holding a [quorum](card:quorum) may elect a primary, and by fencing the old primary so it stops writing.

### Why is failover not instant?

Detection needs several missed health checks, promotion of a standby takes time, and clients keep cached DNS answers until the TTL expires. Amazon RDS Multi-AZ typically takes 60-120 seconds. Size your RTO around this, and use a load balancer rather than DNS when you need a faster switch.
