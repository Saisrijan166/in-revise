## Why it exists

Every server, disk, network link and data centre fails sometimes. If only one copy of a part exists, its failure takes the whole system down.

## How it works

<Steps label="Automatic failover in four steps">
<Step title="Detect">
Health checks or heartbeats fail several times in a row. A short grace period avoids failing over just because a node was busy.
</Step>
<Step title="Decide">
A tool or a person triggers failover. For a whole region, teams often press the button themselves, because a false alarm also costs downtime.
</Step>
<Step title="Promote one">
One standby becomes the new primary. The old primary is fenced off, so two primaries never accept writes.
</Step>
<Step title="Redirect">
The load balancer, a moved IP address or a DNS record now points to the new primary. Clients with a cached DNS answer wait until its TTL runs out.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Compare id="active-passive-vs-active-active" />

- Copies must fail separately: different [zones](card:multi-az) or [regions](card:multi-region), not two servers on one rack.

## Example

How long until users reach the standby with DNS failover? With Amazon Route 53 defaults:

- **Detect:** a check every 30 s, unhealthy after 3 failures in a row, so about 90 s.
- **Redirect:** resolvers may serve the old answer until its TTL ends; AWS advises 60 s or less, so up to 60 s more.
- **Total:** about 2.5 minutes before most clients reach the standby. Plan the RTO around it.

How much spare room does active-active need? Peak load needs 6 servers spread over 3 zones. If one zone fails, only 2 of 3 zones remain, so run 6 × 3/2 = 9 servers (Azure guidance).

## Interview Qs

### How do you remove single points of failure?

List every part on the request path: DNS, load balancer, app servers, cache, database. Run each at least twice, in different zones. Stateless parts go active-active behind a load balancer; the database gets a standby with automatic failover.

### Active-passive or active-active?

Active-passive is simpler and has one writer, but the standby sits idle and failover takes time. Active-active uses all capacity and needs no failover step, but each copy needs spare room and concurrent writes must be reconciled.

### What is split brain, and how do you prevent it?

After a network split, two nodes both act as primary and accept writes, so the data diverges. Prevent it with a majority vote, where only the side with a [quorum](card:quorum) may elect a primary, and by fencing the old primary.

### Why is failover not instant?

Detection needs several missed checks, promotion takes time, and clients keep cached DNS answers until the TTL expires. Amazon RDS Multi-AZ typically takes 60–120 seconds. Size your RTO around it.
