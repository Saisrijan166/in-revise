## Why it exists

Redundancy in one region survives a failed server or zone, but not a region outage, and replicas copy a bad delete or ransomware too. Disaster recovery brings the whole workload back within an agreed data loss and downtime.

## How it works

<Compare id="rpo-vs-rto" />

| Strategy (AWS) | Recovery site has | RPO | RTO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Backup and restore | Backups only; redeploy everything | Hours | 24 hours or less |
| Pilot light | Live data copy; app servers off | Minutes | Tens of minutes |
| Warm standby | Small working copy, always on | Seconds | Minutes |
| Multi-site active-active | Full copies, all serving traffic | Near zero | Potentially zero |

- Cost and complexity grow down the table. Do not pay for a stricter row than the business needs.
- Rebuild the recovery site from infrastructure as code, so recovery does not depend on manual steps.

## Example

**Orders database: RPO 15 minutes, RTO 1 hour.**

- **Nightly backup at 02:00 only:** a failure at 23:00 loses about 21 hours of orders. Fails the RPO.
- **Amazon RDS automated backups:** transaction logs reach S3 every 5 minutes, so a point-in-time restore loses minutes, not hours. Meets the RPO.
- **The restore creates a new DB instance.** Time it in a drill to prove the 1-hour RTO.
- **Region outage:** copy backups to a second region, or run a pilot light there.

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automated backup retention | 7 days (console) or 1 day (API, CLI); 0–35 allowed, 0 turns backups off | Amazon RDS |
| Transaction logs copied to S3 | Every 5 minutes | Amazon RDS |

## Interview Qs

### RPO vs RTO?

RPO is how much data you can afford to lose, measured as time back to the last good copy. RTO is how long you can be down until service is back. Hourly backups mean an RPO of up to one hour.

### Which DR strategy would you choose?

The cheapest one that meets the RPO and RTO: backup and restore for hours, pilot light or warm standby for minutes, multi-site active-active for near zero. Going stricter than needed only adds cost and complexity.

### We have replicas in two regions. Do we still need backups?

Yes. Replication copies every change, including a bad delete, a corrupting bug or ransomware. Point-in-time backups, ideally in another account and region, let you return to before the damage.

### How do you know your DR plan works?

Test it. Restore backups on a schedule, check the data is complete, and time the restore against the RTO. Run failover drills that include the human steps, not just the scripts.
