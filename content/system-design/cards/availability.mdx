## Why it exists

Nothing is up 100% of the time. Teams need a number for "reliable enough", and a rule for when to stop shipping features and fix reliability.

## How it works

| Availability | Downtime a year | Downtime a month (30 days) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 99% | 3.65 days | 7.2 hours |
| 99.9% | 8.76 hours | 43.2 minutes |
| 99.95% | 4.38 hours | 21.6 minutes |
| 99.99% | 52.6 minutes | 4.32 minutes |
| 99.999% | 5.26 minutes | 25.9 seconds |

- **Measure** by time (up time ÷ total time) or by requests (good ÷ total). Google prefers request success rate: big services are rarely fully down.
- **In series** (all parts must work), multiply: 99.9% × 99.9% ≈ 99.8%. **In parallel** (one copy is enough), multiply the failures: 1 − 0.1% × 0.1% = 99.9999%.

## Example

**Error budget.** SLO: 99.9% of requests succeed over 4 weeks. With 3 million requests, 3,000 may fail. A bad deploy fails 2,000, so two-thirds of the budget is gone. If it runs out, releases stop, except urgent and security fixes.

**Recover faster.** MTBF 150 days (3,600 hours), MTTR 1 hour: 3,600 ÷ 3,601 ≈ 99.97%. Cut MTTR to 30 minutes: about 99.986%.

**A real SLA.** Amazon EC2 promises 99.99% monthly uptime per Region, for instances in two or more zones. Below that, customers get 10%, 30% or 100% back as service credits.

## Interview Qs

### What does four nines mean?

99.99% available: about 52.6 minutes of downtime a year, or 4.3 minutes a month. Each extra nine allows 10 times less downtime, and costs far more to reach.

### SLA vs SLO vs SLI?

The SLI is the measurement, like the share of successful requests. The SLO is the target for it, like 99.9% over four weeks. The SLA is the contract with customers, with penalties such as credits; it is usually looser than the internal SLO.

### Two 99.9% services: chained, and redundant?

Chained, both must work: 99.9% × 99.9% ≈ 99.8%. Redundant, one is enough: 1 − 0.001 × 0.001 = 99.9999%. That only holds if they fail independently, for example in different availability zones.

### What is an error budget?

It is 1 minus the SLO: at 99.9%, 0.1% of requests may fail in the window. While budget remains, the team ships changes. Once it is spent, releases pause and reliability work comes first.
