## Why it exists

Replicas must agree on one leader and one order of writes, even when servers crash or the network splits. Without that, two servers can both accept writes and the data forks.

## How it works

<Steps label="Raft leader election">
<Step title="Followers wait for heartbeats">
The leader sends regular heartbeats. A follower that hears nothing for its election timeout, picked at random (for example 150 to 300 ms), assumes the leader is gone.
</Step>
<Step title="Become a candidate">
It adds 1 to the term, the election round number, votes for itself and asks every other server for a vote.
</Step>
<Step title="Win a majority">
Each server votes once per term, and only for a candidate whose log is at least as up to date as its own. A majority of votes makes a leader.
</Step>
<Step title="Lead and replicate">
The leader copies each write to the followers. A write is committed once a majority has stored it.
</Step>
</Steps>

- Two majorities always share a server, so a term can never have two leaders. A cut-off minority cannot elect anyone.
- If votes split, the random timeouts make one candidate retry first, with a higher term.
- **Paxos** (Lamport) solves the same problem with proposers and acceptors; Raft gives the same result in a form that is easier to follow.

## Example

| Servers | Majority | Failures it survives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 2 | 1 |
| 4 | 3 | 1 |
| 5 | 3 | 2 |

A 5-server cluster splits into 3 and 2. The side with 3 elects a leader and keeps committing writes. The side with 2 cannot win a vote or commit anything, so there is no split brain.

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heartbeat interval | 100 ms | etcd v3.6 |
| Election timeout | 1000 ms | etcd v3.6 |
| Quorum size | A majority: n / 2 + 1 servers | Apache ZooKeeper 3.9 |
| Election timeout | 10 s (`electionTimeoutMillis`) | MongoDB replica sets |

## Interview Qs

### How does Raft elect a leader?

A follower that misses heartbeats for a random timeout becomes a candidate: it raises the term, votes for itself and asks for votes. With votes from a majority it becomes leader. Random timeouts make split votes rare.

### What is split brain, and how do you prevent it?

Two nodes both act as leader, usually after a network partition, and accept conflicting writes. Require a majority to lead or commit: only one side of a partition can have one. Use an odd number of nodes, or add a witness vote.

### Why 3 or 5 nodes, and not 4?

A majority must agree. 3 nodes survive 1 failure and 5 survive 2, but 4 nodes need 3 votes and still survive only 1. More nodes also slow writes, because each write must reach more machines.

### Raft vs Paxos?

Both reach agreement through majorities and give the same result. Paxos came first and is known to be hard to understand and build. Raft splits the problem into leader election, log replication and safety, so it is easier to implement; etcd uses it.
