## What it is

Consensus is the problem of getting a group of servers to agree on one value, even when some servers crash or the network between them splits. The value is usually "who is the leader right now" or "what is the next entry in our shared log of writes". A [quorum](card:quorum) counted copies for a single read or write; consensus goes further and keeps the whole group agreeing on a sequence of decisions over time. Leader election is the most common use: the group picks one server to accept writes.

## Why it exists

Replicas must agree on one leader and one order of writes. Without that, two servers can each believe they are in charge, both accept writes, and the data forks into two versions that cannot be merged cleanly. A naive "standby takes over when the leader goes quiet" fails because a cut-off leader is not a dead one, so both could run at once. Consensus fixes this by requiring a majority to agree before anything is decided, and two majorities always share a member, so only one leader can exist at a time.

## How it works

<Steps label="Raft leader election">
<Step title="Followers wait for heartbeats">
The leader sends regular heartbeats. A follower that hears nothing for its election timeout, chosen at random (for example 150 to 300 ms), assumes the leader is gone.
</Step>
<Step title="Become a candidate">
It adds 1 to the term, the election round number, votes for itself, and asks every other server for a vote.
</Step>
<Step title="Win a majority">
Each server votes once per term, and only for a candidate whose log is at least as up to date as its own. A majority of votes makes the candidate the leader.
</Step>
<Step title="Lead and replicate">
The new leader copies each write to the followers. A write is committed once a majority has stored it.
</Step>
</Steps>

Two majorities always share a server, so a single term can never have two leaders, and a cut-off minority can never win a vote. If votes split, the random timeouts make one candidate retry first with a higher term.

## Example

The number of servers decides how many failures the group survives.

| Servers | Majority | Failures it survives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 2 | 1 |
| 4 | 3 | 1 |
| 5 | 3 | 2 |

A 5-server cluster splits into 3 and 2. The side with 3 is a majority, so it elects a leader and keeps committing writes. The side with 2 cannot win a vote or commit anything, so there is no split brain.

## Rules and defaults

- **Majority to decide:** every leader election and every committed write needs floor(N / 2) + 1 servers to agree (Apache ZooKeeper 3.9, Raft).
- **etcd v3.6 timing:** heartbeat interval 100 ms, election timeout 1000 ms; the election timeout should be well above the round-trip time between nodes.
- **MongoDB election timeout:** 10 seconds (`electionTimeoutMillis`) before a replica set starts a new election.
- **Use an odd count:** 3 survives 1 failure, 5 survives 2; an even count adds a node without adding fault tolerance and slows every write.

## Interview Qs

### How does Raft elect a leader?

A follower that misses heartbeats for a random timeout becomes a candidate: it raises the term, votes for itself and asks the others for votes. With votes from a majority it becomes leader. The random timeouts make two candidates starting at once rare, so split votes are uncommon.

### What is split brain, and how do you prevent it?

Two nodes both act as leader, usually after a network partition, and accept conflicting writes. Prevent it by requiring a majority to lead or commit: only one side of any partition can hold a majority. Use an odd number of nodes, or add a witness or arbiter vote.

### Why run 3 or 5 nodes and not 4?

A decision needs a majority. 3 nodes survive 1 failure and 5 survive 2, but 4 nodes need 3 votes and still survive only 1, so the extra node buys no fault tolerance and slows writes, since each write must reach more machines.

### Raft vs Paxos?

Both reach agreement through majorities and give the same guarantees. Paxos came first and is known to be hard to understand and implement. Raft splits the problem into leader election, log replication and safety, which makes it easier to build; etcd uses it.
