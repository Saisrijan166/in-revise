## Why it exists

A payment cut off halfway can charge a customer with no record of it. The app needs the stop signal, and time, to finish cleanly.

## How it works

<Steps label="docker stop on the payment service">
  <Step title="Signal">Docker sends SIGTERM (or STOPSIGNAL) to PID 1.</Step>
  <Step title="Stop taking work">Spring Boot stops accepting new requests.</Step>
  <Step title="Finish and exit">The in-flight ₹500 UPI payment completes; the JVM exits within the grace period.</Step>
  <Step title="Or killed">Still running at the deadline: Docker sends SIGKILL.</Step>
</Steps>

- Use `--init` when the app spawns child processes.
- Exit codes after a kill: [troubleshooting](card:troubleshooting-containers).

## Example

```bash
# tini as PID 1; 40 s > Spring's 30 s per phase
docker run -d --name pay --init --stop-timeout 40 payment-service:1.4.0
```

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `server.shutdown` | `graceful` | Spring Boot 4.1 |
| `spring.lifecycle.timeout-per-shutdown-phase` | 30 s | Spring Boot 4.1 |

## Interview Qs

### Why does my container take 10 seconds to stop?

PID 1 ignored SIGTERM. Usually a shell-form ENTRYPOINT makes `sh` PID 1, which does not pass the signal to java; after the grace period comes SIGKILL. Use exec form, or `exec java ...` in the start script.

### Why is PID 1 special in a container?

The kernel ignores a signal sent to a namespace's init unless it has a handler; only SIGKILL and SIGSTOP from the host get through. PID 1 also adopts orphans and must reap zombies. `--init` puts a tini-based init there to do both.

### How do you stop a Spring Boot container without losing requests?

Exec-form ENTRYPOINT so the JVM gets SIGTERM. Graceful shutdown, on by default, lets in-flight requests finish within 30 s per phase. Set `--stop-timeout` above that.
