## Why it exists

When a service is down, its callers keep waiting, hold threads, and fail too. A breaker stops those calls, so one failure does not cascade.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="Circuit breaker states. CLOSED moves to OPEN when the failure rate passes the threshold. OPEN moves to HALF_OPEN when the wait ends. HALF_OPEN goes back to CLOSED if trial calls succeed, or to OPEN if they fail."
  chart={`stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> CLOSED
  CLOSED --> OPEN: failure rate over threshold
  OPEN --> HALF_OPEN: wait time ends
  HALF_OPEN --> CLOSED: trial calls succeed
  HALF_OPEN --> OPEN: trial calls fail`}
/>

- It opens on a failure rate over many calls, not on one error.
- While OPEN, answer with a [fallback](card:fallback-and-degradation) or a clear error at once.
- The breaker needs a short [timeout](card:timeouts-and-retries) on each call, or it learns about failures too late.

## Example

```java
@CircuitBreaker(name = "inventory", fallbackMethod = "stockUnknown")
public Stock stock(String sku) {
    return inventoryClient.get().uri("/stock/{sku}", sku)
            .retrieve().body(Stock.class);
}

// on failure, or at once while OPEN
private Stock stockUnknown(String sku, Exception e) {
    return Stock.unknown(sku);
}
```

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opens at | 50% failed calls, over the last 100 calls (at least 100 recorded) | Resilience4j 2.x |
| Stays OPEN | 60 s, then 10 trial calls in HALF_OPEN | Resilience4j 2.x |
| While OPEN | throws `CallNotPermittedException` | Resilience4j 2.x |

## Interview Qs

### How do you prevent cascading failures?

A timeout on every call, a few retries with backoff, a breaker per dependency, [bulkheads](card:bulkhead) so one slow service cannot take every thread, and [rate limits](card:rate-limiting) on incoming work. Monitor each dependency's errors and latency.

### Why HALF_OPEN, and not straight back to CLOSED?

A service that is just recovering may fall over again under full traffic. A few trial calls test it gently first.
