## Why it exists

Placing an order spans Order, Inventory and Payment, each with its own database, and [2PC](card:distributed-transactions) is not an option. A saga keeps the data consistent with local steps instead.

## How it works

<Steps label="Order saga when the payment fails">
  <Step title="Order Service">Creates the order with status PENDING.</Step>
  <Step title="Inventory Service">Reserves the stock.</Step>
  <Step title="Payment Service">Charging ₹1,499 fails and it publishes PaymentFailed.</Step>
  <Step title="Inventory Service (compensate)">Releases the reserved stock.</Step>
  <Step title="Order Service (compensate)">Marks the order CANCELLED. On success it would mark it CONFIRMED.</Step>
</Steps>

- Each step publishes its event through an [outbox](card:outbox-and-inbox) and must be [idempotent](card:delivery-guarantees).
- Retry a failing step first; compensate only when it cannot move forward.

## Interview Qs

### How do you handle a transaction across several services?

With a saga: each service commits a local transaction and triggers the next step by event or command. On failure, compensating transactions undo the finished steps.

### Orchestration or choreography?

Orchestration: one orchestrator sends commands and tracks state; clearer for complex flows, but it is one more component that can fail. Choreography: services react to events; fine for short flows, but hard to follow as it grows.

### What if a compensating step fails?

Compensations can fail too, so make them idempotent and retry them until they succeed. If they still fail, flag the saga for manual handling and alert; never assume the data fixed itself.
