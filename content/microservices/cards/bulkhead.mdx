## Why it exists

If all outgoing calls share one pool, a single slow dependency fills it, and calls to healthy services wait behind it.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="The Order service has a 10-slot pool for Payment and another for Inventory. Payment is slow, so its pool is full and an 11th call is rejected; Inventory calls still succeed."
  chart={`flowchart LR
  O[Order service] --> PP["Payment pool: 10 of 10 busy"]
  O --> IP["Inventory pool: 2 of 10 busy"]
  PP --> P[Payment: slow]
  PP -. "11th call" .-> X[Rejected at once]
  IP --> I[Inventory: healthy]`}
/>

- A rejected call goes to a [fallback](card:fallback-and-degradation) or returns a clear error.
- Pair it with a [circuit breaker](card:circuit-breaker) and retries: the bulkhead limits the damage, the breaker cuts the calls off.

## Analogy

<Analogy title="Compartments in a ship's hull" limit="The slow calls still fail; each still needs a timeout.">
A leak floods one compartment, and the ship stays afloat.
</Analogy>

## Example

```yaml
resilience4j:
  bulkhead:
    instances:
      payment:
        maxConcurrentCalls: 10   # the 11th call at the same time is rejected
        maxWaitDuration: 0       # do not wait for a free slot
```

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concurrent calls | 25, no waiting for a slot | Resilience4j 2.x semaphore bulkhead |
| Thread pool | max threads = CPU count; queue of 100 | Resilience4j 2.x thread-pool bulkhead |
| When full | throws `BulkheadFullException` | Resilience4j 2.x |

## Interview Qs

### Semaphore bulkhead or thread-pool bulkhead?

A semaphore caps concurrent calls on the caller's own thread and suits any threading model. A thread pool runs calls on its own threads with a bounded queue and returns a `CompletionStage`, so the caller's thread is not held.

### Where else can you apply the bulkhead idea?

A connection pool per downstream service, separate instances for important clients, separate queues per workload, and containers with their own CPU and memory limits.
