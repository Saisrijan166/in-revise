## Why it exists

Replacing a large system in one rewrite is slow and risky. Moving it piece by piece keeps the shop running and shows value early.

## How it works

<Steps label="Strangling a monolith, one capability at a time">
  <Step title="Facade">Route all traffic through a proxy or [gateway](card:api-gateway); at first it all goes to the monolith.</Step>
  <Step title="Pick">Choose a fairly decoupled capability, for example Payment.</Step>
  <Step title="Build">Write the new service and give it its own data: copy, sync, validate.</Step>
  <Step title="Route">Point that capability's paths at the new service.</Step>
  <Step title="Retire">Delete the old code and tables, then repeat. At the end, remove the facade.</Step>
</Steps>

- When old and new must call each other, an [anti-corruption layer](card:anti-corruption-layer) translates. Keep it to translation; no business rules.
- Rollback stays easy until the legacy tables are removed.

## Example

```yaml
spring:
  cloud:
    gateway:
      server:
        webmvc:                                # Spring Cloud Gateway 5.0
          routes:
            - id: payments-moved
              uri: http://payment-service      # the new service
              predicates: [ "Path=/payments/**" ]
            - id: orders-not-yet
              uri: http://legacy-shop:8080     # still the monolith
              predicates: [ "Path=/orders/**" ]
```

## Interview Qs

### How would you move a monolith to microservices?

Incrementally with the strangler fig: a facade routes traffic, one capability moves at a time with its data, and the old path is retired before the next. Avoid a big-bang rewrite.

### Which service do you extract first?

A fairly decoupled one with few calls back to the monolith, so the team learns to run services at low risk. Next, take parts that change often or matter most.

### What is an anti-corruption layer?

An adapter between two models, from Eric Evans' Domain-Driven Design. It translates calls so the new service never adopts the legacy schema or API.
