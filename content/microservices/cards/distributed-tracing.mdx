## Why it exists

A slow checkout could be slow in any of five services. Logs from each service cannot show the order and timing of the calls; a trace can.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="One trace for a checkout. The root span is the API gateway at 1,640 ms. Its child is the Order Service span at 1,610 ms. The Order Service has two child spans: Inventory Service at 80 ms and Payment Service at 1,500 ms, which is the slow one."
  chart={`flowchart TD
  G["Gateway 1,640 ms (root span)"] --> O["Order Service 1,610 ms"]
  O --> I["Inventory Service 80 ms"]
  O --> P["Payment Service 1,500 ms (slow)"]`}
/>

- The root span covers the whole request; child spans are its steps, so find the deepest slow one.
- Head sampling decides at the start (cheap); tail sampling decides after the trace ends, so it can keep every error.
- Spring Boot 4.1 uses Micrometer Tracing over OpenTelemetry (`spring-boot-starter-opentelemetry`) or Brave: [Spring stack](card:spring-cloud-stack).

## Example

```text
POST /payments HTTP/1.1
Host: payment-service
traceparent: 00-0af7651916cd43dd8448eb211c80319c-b7ad6b7169203331-01
# 00 = version, then trace ID, parent span ID, 01 = sampled
```

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `management.tracing.sampling.probability` | 0.1 (10% of traces) | Spring Boot 4.1 |
| Header it sends | W3C `traceparent` | Spring Boot 4.1 |

## Interview Qs

### Why does a trace split in two at the Payment Service?

The Order Service did not forward the context on that call, for example its RestClient was built by hand, not from the auto-configured builder. Payment then starts a new trace.

### What is OpenTelemetry?

A vendor-neutral CNCF project with APIs, SDKs, the OTLP protocol and a Collector for traces, metrics and logs. It is not a backend; you send the data to Jaeger, Prometheus or a vendor.
