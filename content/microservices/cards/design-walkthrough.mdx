## What it is

This concept is a fixed order for answering a question such as "design an online shop with microservices". It walks the shop from the business flows to deployment, and at each step names the concept that solves that step's problem and the trade-off it brings. It ties the whole tech together, so every step links to a concept you have already read.

## Why it exists

In a design interview it is easy to jump to tools such as Kafka, Redis and Kubernetes, and to forget who owns the data or what happens when a call fails. Interviewers check whether you can connect the ideas and explain trade-offs, not whether you can list products. A fixed order keeps the answer complete and calm, and leaves time for the part that matters most: money and stock.

1. **Requirements:** state the flows (place order, reserve stock, charge ₹500, send confirmation), the scale, and what must be correct at once (the payment) or may happen later (the email).
2. **Boundaries and data:** split by [business capability](card:service-boundaries) into Order, Payment, Inventory and Notification, each with [its own database](card:database-per-service) and no shared tables.
3. **Calls:** choose [sync or async](card:sync-and-async-communication) per call, and put an [API gateway](card:api-gateway) at the edge.
4. **Failures:** give every remote call a [timeout and a bounded retry](card:timeouts-and-retries), a [circuit breaker](card:circuit-breaker) and, where it is safe, a [fallback](card:fallback-and-degradation).
5. **Consistency:** run the order as a [saga](card:saga), publish its events through an [outbox](card:outbox-and-inbox), and charge with an [idempotency key](card:idempotency).
6. **Operations:** [authenticate at the gateway and check in every service](card:microservices-security), add [traces, logs and metrics](card:observability) with [SLOs](card:metrics-and-slos), and deploy on [Kubernetes](card:containers-and-kubernetes) with [probes](card:health-probes), a [canary](card:deployment-strategies) and [more instances](card:scaling-services) under load.

So the key is: each step states a problem first, then the concept that fixes it.

## Example

The deep dive on the money path: the shop's classic failures and what the design does about each.

| Failure | What the design does |
|---|---|
| Payment times out while charging ₹500 | Retry with the same idempotency key, so a charge that went through is not repeated |
| Stock runs out | Inventory reports it, and the saga cancels the order before any money is charged |
| The card is declined | The saga compensates: Inventory releases the reserved stock and the order is cancelled |
| OrderPlaced is delivered twice | Notification and Inventory are idempotent consumers and skip the repeat |

## Common mistakes

- **"Microservices are faster."** They add network hops. What they buy is [independent deploys](card:monolith-and-microservices) and scaling.
- **"Expose each service to clients."** Put an [API gateway](card:api-gateway) in front, so clients have one entry and services can change behind it.
- **"@Transactional covers the payment call."** A local transaction cannot span services; use a [saga](card:saga) with compensations.
- **"The outbox gives exactly-once."** The relay can publish an event twice, so [consumers stay idempotent](card:delivery-guarantees).

## Interview Qs

### Can two services share a code library?

Yes, for small, stable technical code such as logging or messaging helpers, versioned so that each service upgrades on its own. Do not share domain logic or entities: one change then forces both teams to release together, and the services are coupled again.

### How would you design an order, payment and inventory system?

Walk the six steps in order, one or two lines each: flows, boundaries and data, calls, failures, consistency, operations. Then go deep where money and stock meet: the saga, the outbox and the idempotent charge. Say each trade-off aloud, such as eventual consistency for stock.
