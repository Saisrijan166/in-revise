## Why it exists

A remote call is not a method call: the other service may be slow or down. The style decides who waits, and what breaks when one service fails.

## How it works

<Compare id="rest-vs-messaging" />

- Long work over HTTP: answer `202 Accepted` with a status URL, and let the client poll it.
- Events vs commands: [Event-Driven Architecture](card:event-driven-architecture). Queues and topics: [Message Brokers](card:message-brokers).

## Interview Qs

### How do microservices communicate?

Sync HTTP (REST, gRPC) when the caller needs an immediate answer; async messages through a broker such as Kafka or RabbitMQ for events and background work. Most systems use both.

### Is asynchronous communication always better?

No. It removes the need for both services to be up together, but adds a broker, duplicate or late messages, and briefly stale data. A login or stock check needs a sync answer.

### Why is a chain of sync calls a problem?

Each hop adds latency, and if any service in the chain fails or slows, the whole user request fails. Instead, keep a local copy of the data you need, updated by events.
