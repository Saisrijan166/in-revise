## Why it exists

As one app and its team grow, every change waits for one build, one test run and one release. Splitting by business capability lets teams ship on their own.

## How it works

<Compare id="monolith-vs-microservices" />

- **Conway's law:** a system copies its teams' communication structure, so services follow team lines.
- **Monolith first:** boundaries are hard to guess early; moving code between services costs far more than between modules.
- Microservices are a design choice, not just many Spring Boot apps: without clear [boundaries](card:service-boundaries) they become a distributed monolith.

## Interview Qs

### Why do we need microservices if a monolith already works?

Often we do not. They help when many teams must release, scale and own parts independently. The price is network calls, eventual consistency, harder testing and more operations, so a working monolith may be the better choice.

### How do you decide whether to create a new microservice?

Split when a business capability has a clear boundary, its own data and an owning team, and independent release or scaling has real value. Do not split if it would make every operation need many remote calls.

### Are microservices just several Spring Boot apps?

No. Separate apps that share tables or must deploy together are a distributed monolith. Microservices need their own data, stable contracts and independent deployment.
