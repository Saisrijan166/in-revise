## Why it exists

Wiring towns with cable or offices with network links: every point must be connected, and you want to pay for as little cable as possible.

## How it works

<Compare id="kruskal-vs-prim" />

- Both are [greedy](card:greedy): at each step they take the cheapest edge that keeps the result a tree.
- A disconnected graph has no spanning tree; Kruskal then gives one tree per component (a minimum spanning forest).

## Example

```text
Edges: A-B 1, B-C 1, A-C 1.5
MST:   A-B + B-C, total 2 (A-C would close a cycle)
A to C inside the MST costs 2, but the direct edge costs 1.5
-> an MST is not a shortest-path tree
```

## Interview Qs

### Kruskal or Prim?

Both give a minimum spanning tree. Kruskal sorts the edge list and uses union-find, O(E log E). Prim grows from one vertex with a min-heap, O(E log V). Pick the one that fits the input you have.

### How many edges does a spanning tree have?

Exactly V - 1. One fewer leaves a vertex out; one more always creates a cycle.
