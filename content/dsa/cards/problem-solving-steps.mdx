## Why it exists

Under pressure people code too early and get stuck. A routine keeps you moving.

## How it works

<Steps label="Every problem, in this order">
<Step title="Clarify">
Restate it; ask the questions in the points above.
</Step>
<Step title="Work examples">
Solve 2 or 3 small cases by hand; they become your tests.
</Step>
<Step title="Brute force">
Say the simplest correct idea and its time and space cost.
</Step>
<Step title="Optimise the bottleneck">
Find the repeated or slow work and remove it (table below).
</Step>
<Step title="Code">
Compilable code, clear names.
</Step>
<Step title="Test and state the cost">
Dry-run normal and edge inputs, then restate time and space.
</Step>
</Steps>

- Stuck? Draw the input, or solve it by hand and turn your steps into rules.

## Example

| Bottleneck you notice | Move |
| --- | --- |
| Searching for a value again and again | [HashMap or HashSet](card:hashmap-hashset) lookup |
| Recomputing range sums | [Prefix sums](card:prefix-sum) |
| Same subproblem solved many times | Cache it: [dynamic programming](card:dynamic-programming) |
| Sorted input, nested loops over pairs | [Two pointers](card:two-pointers) or [binary search](card:binary-search) |
| Sorting everything to get the top k | [A heap of size k](card:top-k-heap) |

Two Sum: nested loops are O(n²) time; one pass with a HashMap from value to index is O(n) average time and O(n) space.

## Interview Qs

### What do you do first when you get a problem?

Restate it and ask 2–3 clarifying questions (size, range, sorted, duplicates, empty). Then work a small example.

### Should you mention the brute force if you know a better way?

Yes, briefly. It shows you understand the problem and where the bottleneck is.

### What do you do after you finish coding?

Check for off-by-one errors, step through edge cases, restate the cost.
