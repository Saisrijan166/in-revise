## Why it exists

Interview mistakes often come from unstated assumptions, rushed loops and untested boundaries rather than a lack of algorithm knowledge.

## How it works

<Steps label="Live-coding route">
  <Step title="Clarify and plan">Confirm constraints, then name brute force and the better pattern.</Step>
  <Step title="Code the invariant">Explain the state and why each update preserves it.</Step>
  <Step title="Trace and close">Dry-run, check edges, then state time and space.</Step>
</Steps>

- Pause at indices, map updates and loop conditions; those are high-risk lines.

## Interview Qs

### What do you say before coding?

I confirm assumptions, give the brute-force cost, explain the better approach, and state its time and space complexity. This lets the interviewer correct an assumption before the implementation grows.

### Which edge cases do you test first?

Empty input, one element, duplicates, a result at either boundary, and impossible input. The exact set follows the data structure, but those cases expose most off-by-one and state bugs.
