## Why it exists

Interview problems are finite in kind. Matching a clue to a known pattern turns a new problem into an old one you can code.

## How it works

| Clue in the problem | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Sorted array, find a pair or triple | [Two pointers](card:two-pointers) |
| Cycle or middle of a linked list | [Fast and slow pointers](card:fast-slow-pointers) |
| Longest or shortest contiguous subarray or substring | [Sliding window](card:sliding-window) |
| Many range sums, or subarray sum with negatives | [Prefix sum](card:prefix-sum) |
| Count, duplicate, seen before, unsorted pair | [Counting with a HashMap](card:frequency-counting) |
| Next greater or smaller element | [Monotonic stack](card:monotonic-stack) |
| "Minimum possible maximum", answer in a range | [Binary search on the answer](card:binary-search-on-answer) |
| Top k, kth largest, merge k sorted lists | [Top K with a heap](card:top-k-heap) |
| Overlapping ranges, meetings | [Sort the intervals](card:merge-intervals) |
| Grid, islands, connected cells | [Grid BFS and DFS](card:grid-traversal) |
| All subsets, permutations, combinations | [Backtracking](card:backtracking) |
| Count ways or min cost, subproblems repeat | [Dynamic programming](card:dynamic-programming) |
| A local best choice that is provably safe | [Greedy](card:greedy) |

- No clue? Say the brute force and ask what it recomputes ([the steps](card:problem-solving-steps)).

## Interview Qs

### How do you approach a problem you have not seen?

Clarify, work an example by hand, and state the brute force with its cost. Then match the clues to a pattern that removes the repeated work.

### Sorted array: two pointers or binary search?

Finding a pair with a target: two pointers in O(n). Finding one value: [binary search](card:binary-search) in O(log n). A pair can also be found with n binary searches, but that costs O(n log n).
