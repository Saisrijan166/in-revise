## Why it exists

Producers and consumers can crash halfway through a step, so a record may be lost or handled twice. Each semantic is a choice of which risk you accept.

## How it works

<Compare id="at-most-once-vs-at-least-once-vs-exactly-once" />

- Committing after the work: [offset commits](card:offset-commits). Making repeats harmless: [idempotent consumers](card:idempotent-consumers).
- How exactly-once works inside Kafka: [Kafka transactions](card:kafka-transactions).

## Interview Qs

### At-most-once vs at-least-once vs exactly-once?

At-most-once commits first, so a crash loses records. At-least-once processes first, so a crash repeats them. Exactly-once commits output and offsets in one Kafka transaction, so neither happens, but only for Kafka-to-Kafka work.

### How do you get at-most-once on purpose?

Turn off producer retries and commit offsets before processing each batch. It suits data where an occasional loss is fine and a duplicate is not.

### How would you design a reliable Kafka pipeline?

RF 3 with min.insync.replicas 2, producers with acks=all and idempotence, commits only after processing, idempotent consumers, retries then a dead letter topic, and lag alerts: at-least-once with harmless duplicates.
