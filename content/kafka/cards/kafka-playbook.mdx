## What it is

The playbook is the closing summary of this Kafka course. It puts the whole system into one picture of the payments example, lists the rules most questions come back to, and gives a five-step order for answering. It adds no new facts: every row links to the concept that teaches it, so you can revise from here and jump back where you feel unsure.

## Why it exists

Most Kafka questions are the same dozen rules asked in different words. Candidates who know the rules still lose marks by stopping at a definition, or by saying a common half-truth such as "acks=all waits for every replica". One picture, one rule list and one answer order make every answer complete and correct.

1. **What it is:** one plain line, in the payments example. "A partition is one ordered log inside the payments topic."
2. **How it works:** trace the path: the key picks the partition, the leader appends, followers copy, a group member reads and commits its offset.
3. **Guarantee:** say exactly what is promised, such as "order for one orderId", and what is not.
4. **Trade-off:** name the cost, such as "no order across partitions" or "duplicates after a crash".
5. **Failure:** say what happens when a broker, a consumer or a send fails, and what your code adds.

## Example

"How does Kafka keep payment events in order?" answered in five steps:

1. Kafka keeps order inside a partition, not across the whole topic.
2. The Payment service sends with `orderId` as the key, so all events of one order go to one partition and are read in the order written.
3. Guarantee: per-order order, while different orders are processed in parallel.
4. Trade-off: no order across orders, and one very busy key can make its partition hot.
5. Failure: with the idempotent producer, a retried send cannot duplicate or reorder events; a consumer crash replays events in the same order.

## Common mistakes

- **"acks=all waits for every replica."** It waits for every [in-sync replica](card:producer-acks).
- **"The offset is the message id."** It is a position in one partition; [deduplicate on an eventId](card:idempotent-consumers).
- **"More consumers, more speed."** Members beyond the partition count [sit idle](card:consumer-groups).
- **"Kafka is exactly-once end to end."** Transactions cover [only Kafka reads and writes](card:kafka-transactions), not your database or e-mail.
- **"RF 3 means 3 partitions."** It means [3 copies of each partition](card:replication).
- **"The idempotent producer stops double processing."** It stops [only duplicate writes](card:idempotent-producer) from producer retries.
- **"Kafka needs ZooKeeper."** ZooKeeper was removed in Kafka 4.0; Kafka runs on [KRaft](card:kraft).

## Interview Qs

### What are Kafka's main guarantees?

Order within a partition; durability from replication with acks=all and min.insync.replicas; scale through partitions, brokers and consumers; replay, because records are kept until retention removes them; and fan-out, because each consumer group reads the topic on its own.

### Walk me through Kafka end to end.

The producer's key picks a partition; that partition's leader appends the record and followers copy it. When the in-sync replicas have it, it is committed. Each consumer group's members poll their partitions, process the records, then commit offsets, so a restart continues where it stopped.
