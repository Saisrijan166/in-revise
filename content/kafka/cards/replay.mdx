## Why it exists

A bug may have processed payments wrongly, or a new service needs past orders. Because Kafka keeps records after reading, a consumer can read them again instead of asking the producers to resend.

## How it works

| Way | How | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `seek()`, `seekToBeginning()` | Consumer code sets its own position | A start-up rule in the app |
| `offsetsForTimes()`, then `seek()` | Finds the first offset at or after a timestamp | Start from yesterday 00:00 |
| `--reset-offsets` | CLI moves a stopped group: `--to-earliest`, `--to-datetime`, `--shift-by` | Reprocess after a bug fix |
| A new `group.id` | Starts where `auto.offset.reset` says, e.g. earliest | Backfill a new service |

- Make the replaying consumer [idempotent](card:idempotent-consumers): emails and payments must not repeat.
- `auto.offset.reset` is explained in [consumer configs](card:consumer-configs).

## Example

```bash
# Stop every analytics instance first. Without --execute this only previews.
bin/kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --group analytics \
  --topic payments --reset-offsets --to-datetime 2026-10-19T00:00:00.000

# Apply it, then start the instances again
bin/kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --group analytics \
  --topic payments --reset-offsets --to-datetime 2026-10-19T00:00:00.000 --execute
```

## Interview Qs

### How would you reprocess yesterday's payments after fixing a bug?

Deploy the fix, stop the group, reset its offsets with --to-datetime, then restart. Only that group rereads; others are untouched. The consumer must be idempotent so ₹500 is not charged or mailed twice.

### Can a new service read all past orders?

Only what retention still keeps. A new group.id with auto.offset.reset=earliest starts at the oldest kept record. For the full current state, keep a compacted topic.
