## Why it exists

A broker can crash or lose its disk. Copies of each partition on other brokers keep the data safe and the partition available.

## How it works

  alt="Partition P0 of the payments topic. The producer writes to the leader on broker 1. Followers on brokers 2 and 3 fetch from the leader. The consumer reads from the leader."

- Leaders are spread so each broker leads a fair share; leadership returns to the [preferred leader](card:preferred-leader-replica).
- Which followers count as caught up: [ISR](card:isr-and-min-insync). What happens when a leader dies: [fault tolerance](card:fault-tolerance).
- The broker-wide default RF is covered in [topics](card:topics).


## Interview Qs

### Leader vs follower?

The leader handles all writes for its partition and, by default, the reads. Followers copy its log in the same order with the same offsets. An in-sync follower becomes leader if the leader fails.

### Can a consumer read from a follower?

Yes, since Kafka 2.4 (KIP-392). Set `broker.rack` and `replica.selector.class` (RackAwareReplicaSelector) on brokers and `client.rack` on the consumer. It saves cross-zone traffic; records may arrive slightly later.

### How many failures does RF 3 survive?

Up to 2 of the brokers holding the partition can fail before its data is unreachable. Whether writes continue depends on [min.insync.replicas](card:isr-and-min-insync).
