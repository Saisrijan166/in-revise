## Why it exists

"How would you use Kafka here?" has many parts. A fixed order keeps the answer complete; each step links its concept.

## How it works

<Steps label="Designing the shop's events with Kafka">
  <Step title="Events">Name each event and its publisher: OrderPlaced, PaymentCompleted.</Step>
  <Step title="Topics">One [topic](card:topics) per stream: orders, payments.</Step>
  <Step title="Key">Key by the entity whose order matters, here orderId ([keys](card:partition-keys)).</Step>
  <Step title="Partitions">Size from measured rates, with room to grow ([partition count](card:ordering-and-scaling)).</Step>
  <Step title="Durability">RF 3 and acks=all ([replication](card:replication), [acks](card:producer-acks)).</Step>
  <Step title="Consumers">One [consumer group](card:consumer-groups) per service: payment-service, analytics.</Step>
  <Step title="Retention">How long to keep, and who may [replay](card:replay) ([log storage](card:log-storage)).</Step>
  <Step title="Failures">[Retry, then a DLT](card:retries-and-dead-letter-topics); [idempotent consumers](card:idempotent-consumers).</Step>
  <Step title="Schemas">Versioned events with compatibility checks ([schemas](card:schema-registry-and-serialization)).</Step>
  <Step title="Monitor">Alert when [consumer lag](card:consumer-lag) keeps growing.</Step>
</Steps>

- Durability plus failures = the [reliable recipe](card:delivery-semantics).

## Example

100K events/s, with assumed rates you must replace by measured ones:

```text
Target       100,000 events/s x 1 KB = about 100 MB/s in
Assumed p    10 MB/s produced per partition  -> 100 / 10 = 10
Assumed c    2,000 events/s per consumer     -> 100,000 / 2,000 = 50
Partitions   max(10, 50) = 50, sized for 1-2 years of growth
Consumers    up to 50 per group, one per partition
Writes       RF 3 -> about 300 MB/s across the brokers
Disk         100 MB/s x 7 days x 3 copies = about 181 TB
```

## Interview Qs

### The system receives 100K events per second. How would you scale Kafka?

Measure per-partition rates and size partitions from them with headroom. Add brokers for disk and network times RF. One consumer per partition per group, batch and compress, load-test, watch lag.

### How does Kafka achieve scalability?

Partitions spread a topic over brokers, so clients use many brokers at once, and a group's consumers share them. Add brokers for capacity, partitions for parallelism.

### How would you design a reliable event-driven architecture?

Walk the steps above, then go deep on loss and duplicates: the [reliable recipe](card:delivery-semantics), the [outbox](card:transactional-outbox) to publish with the database write, and [idempotent consumers](card:idempotent-consumers).
