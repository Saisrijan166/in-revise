## What it is

The design formula is a fixed order of questions for "How would you use Kafka here?". Each question maps to a concept you have already met: events and topics, the partition key, partition count, replication and acks, consumer groups, retries, retention, schemas and lag. Walking through them in order turns everything learnt so far into one complete design answer.

## Why it exists

A Kafka design question has many moving parts. Without an order, candidates jump to "3 brokers, 100 partitions", forget what must stay in order, and never mention duplicates or lag. Interviewers then probe the gaps. A fixed order makes the answer complete, and each step gives a natural place to say a trade-off.

## How it works

1. Name each event and its publisher, such as `PaymentCompleted` from the Payment service, and give each stream its own [topic](card:topics).
2. Choose the [key](card:partition-keys): the entity whose events must stay in order, here `orderId`.
3. Size the [partitions](card:ordering-and-scaling) from measured rates, with room for one or two years of growth.
4. Set durability: [replication factor](card:replication) 3, [acks=all](card:producer-acks) and [min.insync.replicas=2](card:isr-and-min-insync).
5. Give each service its own [consumer group](card:consumer-groups), then plan failures: [retry, then a DLT](card:retries-and-dead-letter-topics), and [idempotent consumers](card:idempotent-consumers).
6. Finish with [retention and replay](card:replay), [versioned schemas](card:schema-registry-and-serialization), and an alert when [consumer lag](card:consumer-lag) keeps growing.

## Example

"Payments reach 100,000 events per second at a sale. How would you size Kafka?" The per-partition and per-consumer rates below are assumptions to replace with load-test numbers:

```text
Target       100,000 events/s x 1 KB = about 100 MB/s in
Assumed p    10 MB/s written per partition  -> 100 / 10 = 10
Assumed c    2,000 events/s per consumer    -> 100,000 / 2,000 = 50
Partitions   max(10, 50) = 50, plus headroom for 1-2 years of growth
Consumers    up to 50 per group, one per partition
Writes       RF 3 -> about 300 MB/s across the brokers
Disk         100 MB/s x 7 days x 3 copies = about 181 TB
```

What to notice:

- The consumer side needs 50 partitions while the write side needs only 10. The slower side decides, because a group can use at most one consumer per partition.
- Replication multiplies broker writes and disk by the replication factor, so brokers are sized for 300 MB/s, not 100.
- Seven days of retention at this rate needs about 181 TB; shorter retention or compression shrinks it.

## Rules and defaults

- **Measure, do not guess:** `kafka-producer-perf-test.sh` and `kafka-consumer-perf-test.sh` ship with Kafka for load tests.
- **Broker memory:** leave page cache for about 30 seconds of writes (write rate x 30).
- **Headroom up front:** size partitions for growth now, because adding them later moves keys to new partitions ([ordering vs scaling](card:ordering-and-scaling)).

## Interview Qs

### The system receives 100K events per second. How would you scale Kafka?

Measure per-partition and per-consumer rates, and size partitions from the slower side, with headroom. Add brokers for disk and network times the replication factor. Run one consumer per partition per group, batch and compress, load-test, and watch lag.

### How does Kafka achieve scalability?

Partitions spread a topic over brokers, so producers and consumers use many brokers at once, and a group's consumers share the partitions. Add brokers for capacity and partitions for parallelism.

### How would you design a reliable event-driven architecture?

Walk the steps above, then go deep on loss and duplicates: RF 3, acks=all and min.insync.replicas=2 against loss; the [outbox](card:transactional-outbox) to publish together with the database write; at-least-once delivery with [idempotent consumers](card:idempotent-consumers) against duplicates; a DLT for poison records.
