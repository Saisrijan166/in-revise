## Why it exists

More partitions let more consumers work in parallel, but Kafka orders records only inside one partition. Decide which order you really need.

## How it works

<Steps label="Keep each order's events in sequence">
  <Step title="Key by the entity">Send with key orderId; a key maps to one partition ([keys](card:partition-keys)).</Step>
  <Step title="Producer keeps order">The [idempotent producer](card:idempotent-producer) stops retries from reordering a partition.</Step>
  <Step title="One reader per partition">The group gives each partition to one consumer ([groups](card:consumer-groups)).</Step>
  <Step title="Process in order">Do not hand records to a thread pool that can finish a later one first.</Step>
</Steps>

- t is the target throughput; p and c are what one partition handles on the producer and consumer side, measured.

## Example

```bash
# orders has 6 partitions; grow it to 12 (Kafka 4.3 cannot shrink it back)
kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --alter \
  --topic orders --partitions 12
# now hash(orderId) % 12 sends some orders to a new partition
```

## Interview Qs

### How do you guarantee ordering in Kafka?

Only per partition. Key by the entity id, such as orderId, keep the idempotent producer on, and process each partition in order. Global order needs one partition, so one consumer per group.

### How do you choose the number of partitions?

Measure what one partition handles for producing (p) and consuming (c). For target throughput t, use at least max(t/p, t/c), plus headroom, since adding partitions later moves keys.

### Can you reduce a topic's partition count?

No. Kafka does not support it. The usual workaround is a new topic with fewer partitions, then moving producers and consumers to it.
