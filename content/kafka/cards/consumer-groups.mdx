## Why it exists

One consumer cannot keep up with a busy topic, and several services need the same records. Groups split the work inside a service and keep each service's reading separate.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="The payments topic has four partitions. In group payment-service, consumer C1 reads P0 and P1 and consumer C2 reads P2 and P3. In group analytics, consumer A1 reads all four partitions on its own."
  chart={`flowchart LR
  P0[P0] --> C1[payment-service C1]
  P1[P1] --> C1
  P2[P2] --> C2[payment-service C2]
  P3[P3] --> C2
  P0 -.-> A1[analytics A1]
  P1 -.-> A1
  P2 -.-> A1
  P3 -.-> A1`}
/>

- Four partitions, two consumers: two partitions each. Six consumers: four work, two wait.
- Each group keeps its own [committed offsets](card:offset-commits), so a slow group never delays another.
- Exception: in a [share group](card:share-groups), several consumers read the same partition.

## Interview Qs

### A group has 4 partitions and 6 consumers. What happens?

Four consumers get one partition each; two stay idle until a member leaves or partitions are added. To use all six, the topic needs at least six partitions.

### How do two services both read every order?

Give them different group.id values, such as payment-service and inventory-service. Each group receives every record and tracks its own offsets, so each service reads at its own pace.
