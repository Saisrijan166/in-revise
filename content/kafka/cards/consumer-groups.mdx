## What it is

A consumer group is a set of [consumers](card:consumer-basics) that share the same `group.id` and share the work of reading a topic. Kafka gives each partition of the topic to exactly one consumer in the group, so together they read every record once. A different group, with a different `group.id`, reads the same topic again on its own, from its own position.

## Why it exists

One Notification service instance cannot keep up, so you run three. If each read every record, every customer would get three messages. In one group, each partition goes to one instance, so each record is handled once and the work is split. The Analytics service needs every payment too. It uses its own group, so it gets all records without stealing any from Notification and without slowing it down.

1. Each Notification instance starts with `group.id=notification-service` and subscribes to `payments`.
2. Each one finds its group coordinator, a broker chosen for that group, and joins the group. The group is created on the first join; you do not create it beforehand.
3. The partitions are divided among the members: with 4 partitions and 2 consumers, each consumer gets 2. No partition goes to two members of the same group.
4. If there are more consumers than partitions, the extra ones get nothing and wait. They take over if a member leaves.
5. The Analytics service joins with `group.id=analytics`. As the only member, it gets all 4 partitions and tracks its own offsets.

So the partition count decides how many consumers in one group can work at once.

## Example

Three Notification instances run on a `payments` topic with 2 partitions. The group tool lists each member and how many partitions it owns:

```bash
bin/kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 \
  --describe --group notification-service --members
```

```text output
GROUP                CONSUMER-ID                                   HOST            CLIENT-ID       #PARTITIONS
notification-service notify-1-3be5189b-5166-49ec-bbf0-08dbdd96942a /127.0.0.1      notify-1        1
notification-service notify-2-4d02b57c-9977-4ac2-8df1-1f9fb7e3434d /127.0.0.1      notify-2        1
notification-service notify-3-dbfa596c-0312-4c96-a42b-b70de3ec603d /127.0.0.1      notify-3        0
```

What to notice:

- `notify-1` and `notify-2` own one partition each. `notify-3` owns 0: it is connected, but idle.
- To make `notify-3` useful, the topic needs a third partition, not a fourth consumer.
- If `notify-1` stops, the group moves its partition to another member. That move is a [rebalance](card:rebalancing), which you will see soon.

## Rules and defaults

- **group.id:** has no default. It is required when the consumer uses `subscribe()` or saves its offsets in Kafka.
- **Ownership:** inside a consumer group, one partition has at most one consumer at a time. Share groups, covered later in this topic, are the exception.
- **Offsets per group:** each group has its own committed offset for each partition, kept by its coordinator. One group's progress never moves another's.

## Not to be confused with

- **Consumer:** one client that polls and processes records. A group is all the consumers sharing a `group.id`.
- **Topic:** the data being read. The group is the reader, together with where it will resume.

Rule: topic = what; group = who; consumer = one worker in the who.

## Common mistakes

- **"Each instance should get a unique group.id."** Then each instance is a separate group and reads every record, so work is copied instead of split. Instances of one service share one `group.id`.

## Interview Qs

### A topic has 4 partitions and a group has 6 consumers. What happens?

Four consumers get one partition each, and two stay idle. The idle ones take over if an active member leaves. To use all six, the topic needs at least six partitions.

### How do two services both read every payment?

Give them different group.id values, such as notification-service and analytics. Each group receives every record and tracks its own offsets, so each service reads at its own pace and a slow one does not delay the other.

### What happens when a consumer in a group dies?

The group notices it is gone and moves its partitions to the remaining members. The new owner starts each partition at the group's committed offset, so records processed after the last commit are read again.
