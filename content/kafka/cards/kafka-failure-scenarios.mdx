## Why it exists

Interviewers ask "what if this fails?" for every part. Kafka heals some failures itself; the rest are your code's job.

## How it works

| Failure | Kafka does | You do |
|---|---|---|
| [Leader's broker dies](card:fault-tolerance) | Picks a new leader from the ISR | Nothing; clients retry |
| [All in-sync replicas lost](card:fault-tolerance) | Keeps the partition offline (unclean election off) | Bring a replica back, or accept loss |
| [Controller majority lost](card:kraft) | No new leaders or metadata changes | Run 3 or 5 controllers apart |
| [Consumer crashes](card:rebalancing) | Moves its partitions after the session timeout | Make processing idempotent |
| [Consumer too slow](card:consumer-basics) | Removes it after max.poll.interval.ms | Process faster or poll fewer records |
| [Send times out](card:idempotent-producer) | Retries until delivery.timeout.ms | Keep idempotence on; handle the final error |
| [Processing fails](card:retries-and-dead-letter-topics) | Nothing; the next poll() moves past it | Retry in code, then DLT; commit after |
| [Poison record](card:retries-and-dead-letter-topics) | Same failure on every retry | Park it in the DLT and move on |

- Commit only after processing, and a consumer crash causes repeats, not loss ([why](card:delivery-semantics)).

## Interview Qs

### What happens when a Kafka broker goes down?

Each partition it led gets a new leader from its ISR, and producers and consumers refresh metadata and retry. With RF 3 and min.insync.replicas=2, writes with acks=all go on with one broker down.

### What happens if every in-sync replica of a partition fails?

By default the partition stays offline until a safe replica returns, so no committed record is lost. Turning on unclean leader election brings it back sooner, but may drop committed records ([details](card:fault-tolerance)).
