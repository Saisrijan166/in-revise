## What it is

Failure scenarios are the "what happens if this breaks?" questions asked about every part of a Kafka system: a broker, the controllers, a producer's send, a consumer, or one bad record. Each answer has two halves. Kafka heals some failures by itself, such as electing a new leader. Others, such as duplicates or a record that never processes, are your code's job.

## Why it exists

Kafka is chosen because it survives failures, so interviewers test whether you know exactly how far that goes. "Kafka is fault tolerant" is not an answer. The strong answer names what Kafka does, what is lost or repeated, and what you added, such as idempotent consumers or a dead letter topic.

## How it works

| Failure | Kafka does | You do |
|---|---|---|
| [Leader's broker dies](card:fault-tolerance) | Picks a new leader from the ISR | Nothing; clients retry |
| [All in-sync replicas lost](card:fault-tolerance) | Keeps the partition offline (unclean election off) | Bring a replica back, or accept loss |
| [Controller majority lost](card:kraft) | No new leaders or metadata changes | Run 3 or 5 controllers apart |
| [Consumer crashes](card:rebalancing) | Moves its partitions after the session timeout | Make processing idempotent |
| [Consumer too slow](card:consumer-configs) | Removes it after `max.poll.interval.ms` | Process faster or poll fewer records |
| [Send times out](card:idempotent-producer) | Retries until `delivery.timeout.ms` | Keep idempotence on; handle the final error |
| [Processing fails](card:retries-and-dead-letter-topics) | Nothing; the next poll moves past it | Retry, then DLT; commit after |
| [Poison record](card:retries-and-dead-letter-topics) | Same failure on every retry | Park it in the DLT at once |

1. Name what failed: a broker, the controllers, the producer, a consumer, or one record.
2. Say what Kafka does by itself, such as electing a leader or moving partitions.
3. Say what this means for the data: lost, repeated, delayed or blocked.
4. Say what your code adds: idempotent processing, retries with backoff, a dead letter topic, or an alert.

## Example

The Notification service runs two instances. Instance 2 crashes in the middle of a batch:

```text
1. notification-2 polls payments-1, offsets 500-599; last commit: 500
2. it sends receipts for 500-559, then its JVM crashes (no clean shutdown)
3. no heartbeat for the session timeout: the group removes it
4. rebalance: payments-1 moves to notification-1
5. notification-1 starts at the committed offset 500 and reads 500-599 again
```

What to notice:

- Nothing is lost: the commit still pointed at 500, so every unfinished record is read again.
- Records 500-559 are processed twice. Without an [idempotent consumer](card:idempotent-consumers), 60 customers get two receipts.
- Had the service committed before sending the receipts, a crash would skip records instead: at-most-once.

## Common mistakes

- **"acks=all means no failure can lose data."** If every in-sync replica of a partition is lost and someone enables unclean leader election, committed records can be dropped.
- **"A slow consumer is a dead consumer."** A consumer still sending heartbeats is removed only when it fails to call `poll()` within `max.poll.interval.ms`.

## Interview Qs

### What happens when a Kafka broker goes down?

Each partition it led gets a new leader from its ISR, and producers and consumers refresh metadata and retry. With RF 3 and `min.insync.replicas=2`, writes with `acks=all` go on with one broker down.

### What happens when a consumer crashes mid-batch?

After the session timeout the group removes it and rebalances; another member takes its partitions and starts from the last committed offset. Records processed but not committed come again, so processing must be idempotent.

### What happens if every in-sync replica of a partition fails?

By default the partition stays offline until a replica that was in sync returns, so no committed record is lost. Turning on unclean leader election brings it back sooner, but may drop committed records ([details](card:fault-tolerance)).
