## Why it exists

A write is safe only if a copy outlives the leader. Kafka tracks which replicas are caught up, and lets you demand a minimum number of them per write.

## How it works

| RF | min.insync.replicas | Replicas down | acks=all write |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 2 | 0 | OK: all 3 in the ISR must ack |
| 3 | 2 | 1 | OK: 2 in the ISR |
| 3 | 2 | 2 | Fails: NotEnoughReplicas |
| 3 | 3 | 1 | Fails: one loss stops writes |
| 3 | 1 | 2 | OK, but only one copy exists |

- acks=all waits for the current ISR, not every assigned replica: see [producer acks](card:producer-acks).
- The last committed offset is the high watermark: see [offsets](card:offsets).

## Interview Qs

### What is the ISR and how does a replica leave it?

The in-sync replicas: the leader plus followers that keep up. A follower that has not caught up to the leader's log end for `replica.lag.time.max.ms` (30 s), or whose broker loses its controller session, is removed.

### RF 3, min.insync.replicas 2, acks=all: what happens as brokers fail?

One replica down: the ISR has 2, writes go on. Two down: the ISR has 1, so acks=all writes fail with NotEnoughReplicas until a replica catches up. Committed data survives while one in-sync replica lives.

### Does min.insync.replicas matter with acks=1?

Not for the producer: only acks=all writes are rejected when the ISR is too small. Consumers still see a record only once it is on every ISR member and the minimum is met.
