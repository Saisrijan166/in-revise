## Why it exists

A write is only safe once enough brokers hold it, but waiting costs time. `acks` lets each producer choose its point between speed and durability.

## How it works

<Compare id="acks-0-vs-1-vs-all" />

- `acks=-1` is the same setting as `acks=all`.
- How small the ISR may get before writes fail is [min.insync.replicas](card:isr-and-min-insync).
- The full no-loss setup is in [delivery semantics](card:delivery-semantics).

## Defaults

| What | Default | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `acks` | `all` (was `1` before 3.0) | Kafka 4.3 producer |

## Interview Qs

### acks=0 vs acks=1 vs acks=all?

`0`: the producer does not wait, so failures go unseen. `1`: the leader wrote it locally; it is lost if the leader fails before followers copy it. `all`: every in-sync replica has it, the strongest guarantee, with the most latency.

### Can acks=all still lose data?

Yes, if the ISR has shrunk to just the leader: the write is acknowledged with one copy, and losing that broker loses it. Set [min.insync.replicas](card:isr-and-min-insync) to 2 with replication factor 3 to refuse such writes.

### Which acks would you pick for payments?

`acks=all`, the default, so a ₹500 UPI payment event survives a broker crash. `acks=0` or `1` suits only data where losing a few records is acceptable.
