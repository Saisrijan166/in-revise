## What it is

Fault tolerance is Kafka's ability to keep every partition readable and writable, without losing committed records, when brokers fail. It combines the pieces you have met: [replicas](card:replication) on several brokers, the [ISR](card:isr-and-min-insync) of caught-up copies, and the [controller](card:kraft), which picks a new leader. Picking that new leader is called leader election.

## Why it exists

Brokers crash, lose disks, and are restarted for upgrades. If a partition's leader disappeared and nobody replaced it, the Payment Service could not write `PaymentCompleted` events to that partition and consumers could not read them. Kafka replaces the leader automatically, within seconds, and only with a copy that has all committed records.

## How it works

  A["Broker 1 crashes"] --> B["9 s, no heartbeat: fenced"]

  D --> E["Broker 1 returns as follower"]`}

1. Every broker sends a heartbeat to the active controller every 2 seconds. When broker 1 crashes, the heartbeats stop.
2. After `broker.session.timeout.ms` (9 s) with no heartbeat, the controller fences broker 1: it is treated as offline.
3. For every partition broker 1 led, the controller picks a new leader from the remaining ISR and writes the change to the cluster metadata.
4. Producers and consumers get an error from the old leader or time out, fetch fresh metadata, and retry against the new leader.
5. When broker 1 comes back, it catches up as a follower and rejoins the ISR. Later, leadership moves back to it, because it is the preferred leader.

So a crash costs a pause of seconds, not data, as long as one in-sync replica survives.

## Example

Broker 1, the leader of `payments-1`, was killed without a clean shutdown. Fourteen seconds later:

```bash
kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 --describe --topic payments
```

```text output
Topic: payments  TopicId: Kbf63MnoSEGMnJDJk5254g  PartitionCount: 3  ReplicationFactor: 3  Configs: min.insync.replicas=2
  Topic: payments  Partition: 0  Leader: 3  Replicas: 3,1,2  Isr: 2,3  Elr:   LastKnownElr:
  Topic: payments  Partition: 1  Leader: 2  Replicas: 1,2,3  Isr: 2,3  Elr:   LastKnownElr:
  Topic: payments  Partition: 2  Leader: 2  Replicas: 2,3,1  Isr: 2,3  Elr:   LastKnownElr:
```

What to notice:

- Partition 1 is now led by broker 2, which was in its ISR. Two seconds after the crash the same command still showed `Leader: 1`: the controller waits for the session timeout first.
- Broker 1 is gone from every `Isr`, but it is still listed in `Replicas`. It stays an assigned replica and catches up when it restarts.
- Each ISR still has two members, which meets `min.insync.replicas=2`, so acks=all writes continue.

## Rules and defaults

- **Unclean leader election:** `unclean.leader.election.enable` is false (the default since Kafka 0.11). With true, a replica outside the ISR may lead when no in-sync one is alive, and the records it missed are lost.
- **Eligible leader replicas:** since Kafka 4.0 (default for new clusters from 4.1), the controller may also elect a replica that left the ISR but still holds every committed record.
- **Controlled shutdown:** `controlled.shutdown.enable` is true by default. A planned stop syncs the logs and moves the broker's leaderships first, so partitions pause only briefly and no log recovery is needed. It needs RF above 1.
- **Preferred leader:** `auto.leader.rebalance.enable` (true) moves leadership back to the first replica in the list after a broker returns.
- **Racks:** with `broker.rack`, a partition's replicas span as many racks as possible, up to RF.
- **Losing a whole cluster or region:** replication works inside one cluster only; copy topics to another cluster with [MirrorMaker 2](card:mirrormaker2-geo-replication).

## Interview Qs

### How does Kafka achieve fault tolerance?

Each partition has replicas on different brokers, and with `broker.rack` on different racks or zones. The ISR tracks which copies are caught up. When a leader's broker fails, the controller elects a new leader from the ISR. With acks=all and min.insync.replicas 2, every confirmed write is already on at least two brokers.

### What is unclean leader election?

Letting a replica outside the ISR become leader when no in-sync replica is alive. The partition comes back sooner, but the records that replica never copied are lost, even though producers were told they were safe. `unclean.leader.election.enable` has been false by default since Kafka 0.11.

### Why use controlled shutdown?

Before a planned stop, the broker syncs its logs and hands its leaderships to other in-sync replicas. Partitions then switch leaders almost at once, instead of waiting for a session timeout, and the restart needs no log recovery. It only works when partitions have RF above 1.
