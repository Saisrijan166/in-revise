## Why it exists

ZooKeeper was a second system to deploy and configure, and a new controller had to reload all metadata from it. KRaft moves the metadata into Kafka itself.

## How it works

<Compare id="zookeeper-vs-kraft" />

- Metadata changes are events in the [`__cluster_metadata`](card:cluster-metadata-topic) log; brokers fetch new entries from the active controller, like consumers.
- Controllers handle metadata requests; brokers handle data requests.

## Example

One of three controllers (each controller and broker lists all three):

```properties
process.roles=controller
node.id=1
listeners=CONTROLLER://controller1:9093
controller.listener.names=CONTROLLER
controller.quorum.bootstrap.servers=controller1:9093,controller2:9093,controller3:9093
```

## Interview Qs

### What is KRaft, and why did Kafka drop ZooKeeper?

KRaft is Kafka's Raft-based metadata mode: controllers keep metadata in an internal log. It removes a separate system, makes controller failover fast because standbys already hold the state, and supports more partitions.

### How many controllers do you run?

Three or five. A majority must stay up: three survive one failure, five survive two. Keep them separate from brokers in production.

### How do you move a ZooKeeper cluster to Kafka 4.x?

Migrate it to KRaft first on the bridge release, Kafka 3.9, then upgrade. A 4.x broker cannot start in ZooKeeper mode.
