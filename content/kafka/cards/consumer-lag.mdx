## What it is

Consumer lag is how many records a consumer group still has to read. For one partition it is the log-end offset, where the next record will be written, minus the group's [committed offset](card:offset-commits). The group's total lag is the sum over its partitions. Lag 0 means the group has caught up.

## Why it exists

The Payment service can write faster than Analytics can process. Without a number for the gap, you find out when reports are an hour late, or when [retention](card:log-storage) deletes records nobody read. Lag shows the gap early. It also shows Kafka's backpressure: a slow reader does not slow the writers, because the log stores the backlog and the reader falls behind instead.

## How it works

| Cause | What to do |
|---|---|
| Slow processing per record | Make it faster, for example batch the database writes |
| Slow database or API downstream | Bulk calls, caching; `pause()` partitions while it recovers |
| Fewer consumers than partitions | Add consumers, up to the [partition count](card:consumer-groups) |
| Every partition already has a consumer | Add partitions ([ordering and scaling](card:ordering-and-scaling), later) |
| Frequent rebalances | Fix poll timeouts, use static membership ([rebalancing](card:rebalancing)) |

1. The Payment service writes 1,000 records a second to `payments`.
2. Analytics processes 800 a second, so its lag grows by about 200 a second. The records wait in the log.
3. Monitoring reads the lag, with `kafka-consumer-groups.sh` or the client metric `records-lag-max`, and alerts when it keeps growing.
4. You find the cause with the table above and fix it in that order: the slow work first, then more consumers, then more partitions.
5. If the lag is never fixed and records pass the retention time, they are deleted unread. The group's offset is then out of range, and [auto.offset.reset](card:consumer-configs) decides where it restarts.

## Example

Analytics read 6 of 10 payments and stopped. The group tool shows where it is:

```bash
bin/kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server localhost:9092 \
  --describe --group analytics
```

```text output
Consumer group 'analytics' has no active members.

GROUP           TOPIC           PARTITION  CURRENT-OFFSET  LOG-END-OFFSET  LAG             CONSUMER-ID     HOST            CLIENT-ID
analytics       payments        0          5               5               0               -               -               -
analytics       payments        1          1               5               4               -               -               -
```

What to notice:

- `CURRENT-OFFSET` is the committed offset. On partition 1 it is 1 and `LOG-END-OFFSET` is 5, so `LAG` is 5 − 1 = 4.
- Partition 0 has caught up and partition 1 has not. Lag is per partition, so always look at the rows, not only the total.
- "No active members" means no Analytics consumer is running. The lag stays, and grows with new payments, until one starts.

## Rules and defaults

- **Which offset:** the tool uses the committed offset. The client metric `records-lag-max` uses the consumer's position, so it can be lower than the tool's number.
- **Slowing one partition:** `pause()` stops fetching from chosen partitions while the consumer keeps polling and stays in the group; `resume()` starts them again.
- **Share groups:** have their own tool, `kafka-share-groups.sh --describe`, which also shows lag.

## Interview Qs

### What is consumer lag and how do you monitor it?

It is the gap between the newest offset in a partition and the group's committed offset. Check it with kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe or the records-lag-max metric, and alert when it keeps growing over minutes, not on one spike after a burst.

### What causes lag, and how do you fix it?

Slow processing, slow downstream calls, too few consumers, too few partitions, or frequent rebalances. Fix the slow part first, then add consumers up to the partition count, then add partitions. More consumers than partitions never helps.

### How does Kafka handle backpressure?

The consumer pulls, so Kafka never forces records on a slow reader. Unread records wait in the log for the retention time, and the gap shows up as lag. Producers keep writing at full speed. The risk is lag growing past retention, which loses records for that group.
