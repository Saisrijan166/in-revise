## What it is

Kafka security is three separate layers. Encryption (TLS) hides the bytes on the network. Authentication proves who a client is: with SASL, a framework for logins such as a username and password or a token, or with mutual TLS, where the client shows a certificate. Authorisation then decides what that identity, called a principal, may do, using ACLs (access control lists) such as "payment-service may write to payments".

## Why it exists

Out of the box, a broker accepts any client on its port with no login and no encryption. Anyone who reaches the network can read the payments topic in plain text, or write fake `PaymentCompleted` events. A production cluster must encrypt traffic, know which service is connecting, and limit each service to its own topics.

## How it works

| Layer | In Kafka | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Encrypt in transit | TLS on the listener | `security.protocol=SSL` or `SASL_SSL` |
| Authenticate | Mutual TLS, or a SASL mechanism | `ssl.client.auth=required`, `sasl.mechanism` |
| Authorise | ACLs per principal | `authorizer.class.name=…StandardAuthorizer` |
| Limit | Client quotas on bytes and requests | per user or `client.id` |

1. The client connects to a TLS listener, and the two sides set up an encrypted channel.
2. The client authenticates, for example with SCRAM, and the broker maps it to a principal such as `User:payment-service`.
3. For every request, the authorizer checks the ACLs: is this principal allowed this operation on this topic or group?
4. If no ACL allows it, the request fails with an authorisation error, unless the principal is a super user.

## Example

The Payment service connects with SCRAM over TLS:

```properties
bootstrap.servers=broker1:9093
security.protocol=SASL_SSL
sasl.mechanism=SCRAM-SHA-512
sasl.jaas.config=org.apache.kafka.common.security.scram.ScramLoginModule required \
  username="payment-service" password="<from the secret store>";
ssl.truststore.location=/etc/kafka/truststore.jks
```

What to notice:

- `SASL_SSL` means the SCRAM login and all records travel inside TLS. With `SASL_PLAINTEXT`, the same login works but the data is unencrypted.
- The user name becomes the principal `User:payment-service`, which ACLs refer to, so each service should have its own user.
- The truststore holds the certificate authority that signed the brokers' certificates. If that authority is a private one and the truststore is missing, the client cannot trust the broker and the connection fails.
- The password comes from a secret store, never from source code.

## Rules and defaults

- **Default listener:** `PLAINTEXT://:9092` in Kafka 4.3; clients default to `security.protocol=PLAINTEXT`.
- **Protocols:** `security.protocol` is `PLAINTEXT`, `SSL`, `SASL_PLAINTEXT` or `SASL_SSL`; only `SSL` and `SASL_SSL` encrypt.
- **KRaft authorizer:** `org.apache.kafka.metadata.authorizer.StandardAuthorizer`, set on brokers and controllers; super users are listed in `super.users`.
- **Consumer ACLs:** Read and Describe on the topic, plus Read on the group. `kafka-acls.sh --add --consumer` with `--topic` and `--group` adds them.
- **At rest:** Kafka writes log segments as plain bytes; encrypt the disks or the payloads yourself.

## Interview Qs

### How do you secure a Kafka cluster?

Encrypt with TLS listeners, authenticate every client and broker with SASL (SCRAM, OAUTHBEARER, Kerberos) or mutual TLS, authorise with ACLs, and add quotas so one client cannot starve others. Encrypt disks separately, because Kafka does not.

### What ACLs does a consumer need?

Read and Describe on the topic, and Read on its consumer group. `kafka-acls.sh --add --consumer`, with the principal, `--topic` and `--group`, creates them in one command.

### What is the difference between SASL_PLAINTEXT and SASL_SSL?

Both authenticate the client with SASL. `SASL_PLAINTEXT` then sends the login exchange and all data unencrypted; `SASL_SSL` runs everything over TLS. Use `SASL_SSL` anywhere outside a test setup.
