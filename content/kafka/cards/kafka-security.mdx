## Why it exists

Out of the box, anyone who reaches a broker can read the payments topic in plain text. Production clusters need encryption, a login and per-topic permissions.

## How it works

| Layer | In Kafka | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Encrypt in transit | TLS on the listener | security.protocol=SSL or SASL_SSL |
| Authenticate | Mutual TLS, or a SASL mechanism | ssl.client.auth=required, sasl.mechanism |
| Authorise | ACLs per principal | authorizer.class.name=…StandardAuthorizer |
| Limit | Client quotas on bytes and requests | per user or client.id |

- security.protocol is PLAINTEXT, SSL, SASL_PLAINTEXT or SASL_SSL; only SSL and SASL_SSL encrypt.
- With the authorizer on, a resource with no ACL is open only to super users.

## Example

The Payment Service connects with SCRAM over TLS:

```properties
bootstrap.servers=broker1:9093
security.protocol=SASL_SSL
sasl.mechanism=SCRAM-SHA-512
sasl.jaas.config=org.apache.kafka.common.security.scram.ScramLoginModule required \
  username="payment-service" password="<from the secret store>";
ssl.truststore.location=/etc/kafka/truststore.jks
```

## Interview Qs

### How do you secure a Kafka cluster?

Encrypt with TLS listeners, authenticate every client and broker with SASL (SCRAM, OAUTHBEARER, Kerberos) or mutual TLS, authorise with ACLs, and add quotas so one client cannot starve others. Encrypt disks separately.

### What ACLs does a consumer need?

Read and Describe on the topic, and Read on its consumer group. `kafka-acls.sh --add --consumer`, with the principal, `--topic` and `--group`, creates them.
