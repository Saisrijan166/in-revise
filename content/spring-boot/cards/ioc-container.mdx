## Why it exists

When every class builds its own collaborators with `new`, classes are tied together and hard to test. The container takes over creating and connecting objects.

## How it works

<Mermaid
  alt="Application starts. Spring creates the ApplicationContext. It collects bean definitions from scanned @Component classes, @Bean methods and auto-configuration. It creates the singleton beans, injects their dependencies, wraps some in proxies, and the beans are ready."
  chart={`flowchart TD
  S([App starts]) --> C[ApplicationContext created]
  C --> D["Bean definitions: scanned classes, @Bean methods, auto-configuration"]
  D --> B[Create singleton beans]
  B --> I[Inject dependencies]
  I --> P[Add proxies where needed]
  P --> R([Beans ready])`}
/>

- In Spring Boot, [startup](card:application-startup) creates the context, and [auto-configuration](card:auto-configuration) adds its own beans.
- An object you create with `new` is not a bean: Spring injects nothing into it and does not manage it.
- Proxies are what make `@Transactional` and similar annotations work: see [proxies](card:aop-proxies).

## Interview Qs

### What is IoC, and what is the IoC container?

Inversion of control means your code does not create its collaborators; the container does. In Spring the container is the ApplicationContext: it creates beans, injects their dependencies and manages their lifecycle.

### What is a Spring bean?

An object that the IoC container creates, wires and manages. An object you create yourself with `new` is not a bean.

### BeanFactory vs ApplicationContext?

BeanFactory is the basic container that creates and wires beans. ApplicationContext extends it with events, i18n messages, AOP support and automatic post-processor registration. Use ApplicationContext; Spring Boot always creates one.
