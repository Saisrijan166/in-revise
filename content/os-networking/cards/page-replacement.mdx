## Why it exists

RAM holds only some of the pages in use. When a new page needs a frame, one must leave; a bad choice means more slow disk reads.

## How it works

| Policy | Evicts | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| FIFO | The page loaded first | Simple, ignores use; suffers Belady's anomaly |
| LRU | The least recently used page | Good; exact tracking is too costly |
| Optimal | The page used farthest in the future | Needs the future: only a benchmark |
| Clock | First page whose use bit is 0, clearing bits on the way | Cheap LRU approximation |

- Swap space itself: [virtual memory](card:virtual-memory).

## Example

Three frames, ten page references (F = fault):

```text
refs     7 0 1 2 0 3 0 4 2 3
FIFO     F F F F . F F F F F   9 faults
LRU      F F F F . F . F F F   8 faults
Optimal  F F F F . F . F . .   6 faults
```

## Interview Qs

### What is thrashing and how do you fix it?

The processes' working sets do not fit in RAM, so the system spends its time paging and gets little done. Add RAM, run fewer processes, or reduce memory use.

### What is Belady's anomaly?

With FIFO, more frames can cause more faults. For 1 2 3 4 1 2 5 1 2 3 4 5, FIFO faults 9 times with 3 frames and 10 times with 4.

### Why not use the optimal policy?

It must know which page is used farthest in the future, which the OS cannot know. It is the yardstick for judging real policies.
